# Axialent Coaching Handbook Summary

> **Source:** "La empresa consciente - Manual de coaching" by Fred Kofman, v1.0 March 2013
> **Purpose:** Reference for AI Coach design — this is the official Application Coaching methodology

---

## Core Philosophy

### Development Model vs Defective Model

The handbook explicitly rejects the "defective model" where coaches are experts who fix broken people. Instead, it adopts a **development model**:

- Coachees have the capacity to grow and develop wisdom to respond to challenges
- Coach is an expert in *process*, not content — helps coachee explore and face challenges effectively
- Coach's job is NOT to "fix" but to support the coachee in perceiving clearly, making significant decisions, and implementing them
- The coachee's story, no matter how logical, is often what's keeping them stuck

> "Nuestro trabajo como coaches no es arreglar sino ayudar a crecer."

## Chapter 1: The 8 Steps of a Coaching Conversation

The pattern is not rigid — steps may blend, overlap, or take different prominence depending on the conversation.

### 1. Preparación (Preparation) — 15 min before

**Three tasks:**
1. **Prepare yourself** — review coachee info, then empty your mind, do centering routine
2. **Prepare the session** — logistics (time, place, quiet private space)
3. **Prepare the participant** — send intro email explaining purpose, ask them to think of a situation to work on

### 2. Apertura (Opening) — 5 min

**Objectives:**
- Build connection with participant
- Establish friendly, trusting atmosphere
- Facilitate openness to explore difficult issues

**Key actions:**
- Ask about their workshop experience (if applicable)
- Explain coaching process and confidentiality
- Verify they're available for the full session
- Look for points of affinity to build rapport quickly

**For follow-up sessions:** Reference previous conversation, check on developments since last meeting.

### 3. Comprensión (Understanding) — 10-20 min

**The Gap (La Brecha):**
> "Si no hay brecha, no hay coaching."

The gap is the difference between current state and desired state. It can be:
- A challenge without a satisfactory response
- An undesirable situation they're trying to overcome
- Something they want to improve but don't know how
- A vision they want to realize but feel unsure how
- Something about themselves, others, or the task they want to understand better

**Key questions to define the gap:**
- What are the facts involved?
- What does the participant think about the situation and people involved?
- How do they interpret these facts?
- How do they feel about it? What are their concerns?
- What do they want to do about it? What change do they want?
- What do they hope to achieve?
- What have they tried that hasn't worked? Where are they stuck?
- What would they like to learn?

**Critical insight:** The coachee's story is exactly what's keeping them stuck. Don't believe it without careful examination. Unproductive thinking patterns are embedded in their interpretation.

**Technique: Role Play for Data Collection**
Ask the coachee to dramatize key conversations as they happened. The third-person account is often biased compared to first-person dramatization. This reveals raw data less filtered by mental models.

**Conclude this step by:**
1. Summarizing the gap as you understand it
2. Validating the participant's experience (it's reasonable to feel this way)
3. Expressing confidence you can help

### 4. Contrato (Contract) — 10 min

**Three objectives to agree on:**

| Objective | Definition |
|-----------|------------|
| **Product Objective** | The conditional outcome they want (depends on external factors) |
| **Process Objective** | The unconditional objective — what they can do regardless of outcome |
| **Coaching Objective** | What you'll specifically do in THIS session |

**The Process Objective is critical.** Example:
- Product: "I want my boss to approve my transfer to Frankfurt"
- Process: "I want to explain my situation clearly, understand my boss's perspective, and negotiate in a way that works for both of us"

The process objective = pursuing the product objective with effectiveness AND integrity.

**Typical process objectives for difficult conversations:**
1. Understand the other party's perspective (their perception, ideas, feelings, desires)
2. Express your own perspective
3. Negotiate a mutually beneficial way forward (or implement BATNA)

**For the coaching objective:** "We have 40 minutes. What's the most effective way to use our time?"

### 5. Resolución (Resolution) — 15-30 min

**Outcomes:**
- Higher level of consciousness
- Higher level of skill
- An action plan

**Favorite tool: Role plays** (see Chapter 2)

**Common problems people bring (covers 90%+ of cases):**

**Attitude issues:**
- Victim vs protagonist mindset
- Respecting others as protagonists vs trying to control them
- Pursuing unconditional process objectives vs fighting only for conditional outcomes
- Recognizing incomplete information, possible bias in interpretations
- Self-care and honoring need for rest

**Behavior issues:**
- Expressing truth authentically and respectfully (not "brutally honest" or "politely hypocritical")
- Receiving others' perspectives with open mind
- Resolving conflicts with win-win perspective
- Managing escalations cleanly (implementing BATNA while preserving relationship)
- Handling internal dilemmas (honoring both parts of yourself)
- Making impeccable commitments (requests, responses, managing fulfillment, apologies, complaints)

**Typical resolution process:**
1. Brainstorm strategies to pursue process objective
2. Validate some options, confront others, propose alternatives
3. Select one to try
4. Direct role play — participant tests strategy, coach receives and experiences impact
5. Coach gives feedback or inverts roles to show what participant did
6. Discuss effectiveness and alignment with values
7. If solid, practice variations with increasing difficulty
8. If flawed, return to step 2
9. Coach may do "Houdini" role play — demonstrating a possible approach
10. If participant accepts the model, explain what you did and why
11. Participant practices in direct role play, coach increases difficulty progressively
12. When comfortable with strategy and skill level → move to Commitment

### 6. Compromiso (Commitment) — <5 min

**Definition of learning:** Acquiring a skill to achieve a result that wasn't possible before.

**Questions to generate commitment:**
1. "¿Lo harías?" (Would you do it?)
2. "¿Lo harás?" (Will you do it?)
3. "¿Para cuándo?" (By when?)
4. "¿Me avisas qué pasó antes de X?" (Will you let me know what happened before X?)

**If participant isn't ready** for full action, ask what they ARE ready to do. Even small steps count.

**Request to report back serves three purposes:**
1. Opportunity to reflect on consequences
2. Safety net for learning if something goes wrong
3. Accountability mechanism

**Key:** Offer that if they change their mind, that's OK — but still contact you to discuss. The coach represents the coachee's higher self.

### 7. Cierre (Closing) — 5 min

**Steps:**
1. **Learnings** — "What's on your mind right now?" "What's the lesson in this for you?"
2. **Extension** — Generalize learnings to other situations, skills, beliefs
3. **Anchoring** — Create a trigger to remember (note, physical gesture, deep breath)
4. **Feedback for coach** — "What worked well?" "What would you change for next time?"
5. **Future coordination** — When/how will you meet again
6. **Appreciation** — Recognize something genuine about their work

Let the participant have the last word.

### 8. Seguimiento (Follow-up) — 10 min after

**Registro (Notes):**
- Write impressions immediately after session
- Record commitment and date they'll report back
- If they don't report, send a gentle inquiry
- Notes inform preparation for next session

**Follow-up email (1-2 days after):**
- Appreciation for the work together
- Summary of key lessons
- Your understanding of their commitment and when they'll update you
- Reiteration that you're available to help

## Chapter 2: Role Plays (Juegos de Rol)

Role plays are fundamental to Application Coaching. They offer a non-prescriptive way to show tools, test impact of behavior, and suggest new approaches.

### Two Types

| Type | Description | Purpose |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Direct (1st person)** | Coachee plays themselves, coach plays counterpart | Connect emotionally, gather information, practice |
| **Inverse (2nd person / "Houdini")** | Coach plays coachee, coachee plays counterpart | Experience other's perspective, model new behaviors |

### Direct Role Play Uses

1. **Connect emotionally** — "What do you feel before the conversation? What are you trying to achieve?"
2. **Inform** — Coach observes what coachee actually does (vs what they say they did)
3. **Practice** — Test different strategies with increasing difficulty levels

### Inverse Role Play Uses

1. **Experience other's perspective** — "How does it feel to be on the other side?"
2. **Model conscious behavior** — Coach demonstrates best version of coachee ("Houdini")
3. **Experience own behavior from other's perspective** — After practicing, see how it lands

**Warning:** Sometimes coachees portray their counterpart as a monster, validating their belief that "nothing can be done." If suspected, pause and ask if this is really how the person is on a "good day."

## Chapter 3: Centrarse (Centering)

Centering allows complete presence in the here and now. It's fundamental for excellent performance in any discipline.

### Four Modes of Centering

1. **Foundations** — Long-term practices (meditation, yoga, journaling, etc.)
2. **Preparation** — Just before session
3. **Re-centering** — During session (periodic deep breathing)
4. **Letting go** — After session (writing notes as cleansing ritual)

### Six Centering Practices

| Practice | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Connection with values** | Know your core values and mission as coach |
| **Relaxation** | Release tension, achieve "relaxed alertness" |
| **Breathing** | Conscious breathing is simplest, most powerful way to center |
| **Emptying** | Empty your cup to receive — leave aside concerns about yourself |
| **Dual attention** | 50% attention inside, 50% outside — aware of self AND participant |
| **Rehearsal** | Visualize successful session before it happens |

**"Relaxed Alertness"** (alerta relajada) is the optimal state for coaching.

## Chapter 4: Recibir (Receiving)

Once centered and "empty," you have space to fully receive the coachee and their story. Being completely heard, seen, and felt — without reservations or judgments — is one of the most transformative experiences for a human being.

### Ten Receiving Disciplines

| Discipline | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| **Prestar atención (Attending)** | Full attention — eye contact, open body, concentration, silence, vocal qualities, verbal following |
| **Observar (Observing)** | Notice non-verbal and verbal behavior, identify discrepancies/incongruences |
| **Construir buena relación (Building rapport)** | Match and mirror behavior (acompañamiento), then gently lead |
| **Escuchar (Listening)** | Alentar (encourage), parafrasear (paraphrase), resumir (summarize) |
| **Clarificar (Clarifying)** | Help coachee articulate more precisely |
| **Validar (Validating)** | Acknowledge their experience makes sense |
| **Indagar (Inquiring)** | Ask questions to deepen understanding |
| **Reflejar (Reflecting)** | Mirror back feelings and values |
| **Posicionamiento pentagonal** | Five positions perspective-taking |
| **Tomar notas (Note-taking)** | Capture key points without losing presence |

### Ordinary Listening vs Coach Listening

**Ordinary listening:**
- Preparing your response
- Seeking confirmation of expectations
- Listening for what's wrong
- Waiting your turn to talk
- Agreeing/disagreeing
- Looking for something to fix
- Judging
- Self-referencing

**Coach listening:**
- Setting aside preconceptions and judgments
- Understanding their perspective — how they see the world and why
- Listening for values, mental models, attitudes, beliefs
- Observing the whole picture, not just words

## Key Concepts for AI Coach Design

### The Gap (La Brecha)
The fundamental unit of coaching work. No gap = no coaching. The AI must help identify and define the gap clearly.

### Product vs Process Objectives
Product objectives depend on external factors. Process objectives are unconditional — what the person can do regardless of outcome. The coach helps shift focus from product-only to product + process.

### The Development Model
The AI coach should never position itself as the expert who fixes. It should position as a facilitator who helps the coachee grow and develop their own capacity.

### Role Plays
A powerful tool for the resolution phase. In AI context, this could be:
- Guiding the user through simulated conversations
- Having the AI play the counterpart for practice
- Switching perspectives ("How do you think they experienced that?")

### Session Structure
Each session has a clear arc:
1. Open and connect
2. Understand the gap
3. Contract what we'll work on
4. Resolve (increase consciousness, skill, create plan)
5. Commit to action
6. Close with learnings and anchoring
7. Follow up

### Multi-Session Continuity
The handbook emphasizes:
- Referencing previous sessions in opening
- Recording commitments and following up
- Building on accumulated context
- The relationship develops over time

## Application to AI Coach

### What the AI Can Do

1. **Guide through 8 steps** — Not rigidly, but as a flexible structure
2. **Identify the gap** — Through questioning, not assuming
3. **Distinguish product vs process objectives** — Help user focus on what they can control
4. **Use Conscious Business frameworks** — Mindsets, difficult conversations, agreements, feedback
5. **Facilitate perspective-taking** — "How do you think they saw it?"
6. **Generate commitments** — "What will you do? By when? Will you report back?"
7. **Follow up** — Between sessions, check on commitments
8. **Validate and reflect** — "It makes sense that you'd feel that way"

### What Requires Human Element (or Advanced Design)

1. **Role plays** — Especially "Houdini" where coach models behavior
2. **Reading non-verbal cues** — Limited to text analysis
3. **Dual attention** — AI cannot truly experience the session
4. **Centering** — AI doesn't have internal state (though it can guide user's centering)
5. **Rapport building** — Physical mirroring not possible, but linguistic rapport is

### Design Implications

1. **Session structure should be flexible** — Follow the energy, not the script
2. **The gap is sacred** — Don't start resolution until gap is clear and contracted
3. **Process objectives are key** — Always help user identify what's in their control
4. **Don't be the expert** — Ask, don't tell
5. **Commitments create accountability** — Build in follow-up mechanisms
6. **The story is often the problem** — Gently challenge interpretations
