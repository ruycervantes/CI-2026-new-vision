### The Opening Grammar (CASO 2)

The pattern reveals a less consistent grammar than Ruy's 4-session case:

```
LSI Debrief: Background + Diagnostic exploration + Initial gap identification
CS#1:        Coachee-initiated reflection (extends LSI) + Exploration + Commit
CS#2:        Commitment follow-up + New theme + Exploration
CS#3:        Unstructured reflection (no gap, no commitment) + Coach waits
CS#4:        Coachee requests memory recap + Theme emerges mid-session
CS#5:        Thematic continuation + Teaching (ITC) + Reluctant commitment
CS#6:        Commitment follow-up + Engagement balance + Convergence
```

**Key observations:**

1. **The CS#3-CS#4 dip.** Sessions 3 and 4 both open with low coachee energy and no clear thread. CS#3 has no concrete gap. CS#4 requires the coach to provide direction via memory recap. This is a "middle of the engagement" phenomenon -- the initial momentum from the LSI and CS#1-2 has been consumed, the surface issues (feedback, pedidos) have improved, and the deeper work has not yet emerged. This is exactly where theme graduation should happen, but COACHEE2's case shows it can stall.

2. **Commitment follow-up is the strongest opening mechanism.** CS#2 and CS#6 -- the sessions with explicit commitment follow-up -- have the strongest openings. CS#3 and CS#4 -- sessions without commitment threads -- have the weakest. This confirms the CASO 1 finding that commitments are the primary connective tissue between sessions.

3. **The coach's memory functions as a safety net.** When COACHEE2 arrives directionless in CS#4, Horacio's ability to recap prior themes (Motivation, Compromisos, Career growth) provides structure. Without this memory, the session could have floundered. This is the moment where "one long conversation" depends entirely on the coach's notes.

4. **Self-initiated openings signal engagement.** CS#1's opening -- where COACHEE2 arrives already reflecting on the LSI gap -- is the strongest signal of coaching engagement in the early process. The coachee has been thinking between sessions without being assigned homework.

### How Coach Handles Non-Completion and Avoidance

1. **CS#5: "Proceso que va a tomar tiempo."** When COACHEE2 frames procrastination change as a long process (which is true but also evasive), Horacio does not accept it as sufficient. He "indago mas a fondo" and "insistio sutilmente" to anchor the commitment in a specific case. This is the clearest example of the coach refusing to let the coachee leave without a concrete commitment. The handbook's "if they're not ready for full action, ask what they ARE ready to do" is partially visible here, but Horacio pushes harder than the handbook suggests -- he presses for a real behavioral trial, not just a reduced commitment.

2. **CS#2/CS#3: Missing commitments, missing follow-up.** When sessions end without commitment (CS#2) or with a soft suggestion (CS#3), the next session opens weaker. Horacio does not retroactively create accountability -- he does not say "last time we didn't set a specific commitment, so..." Instead, the cost is structural: the session opening lacks a thread.

3. **"Tuvo suerte" -- luck attribution as avoidance.** In CS#2, COACHEE2 attributes Martin's positive response partly to luck. Horacio flags this internally (yellow light) but does not challenge it in the moment. This is notable because in Ruy's case, Horacio's "outlier vs design" reframe ("was this luck or intentional?") was a key challenge intervention. Here, the coach chose not to deploy it, perhaps because the trust level was different or the coachee's sensitivity to "desmotivar" suggested the challenge could backfire.

### Theme Graduation

CASO 2 demonstrates theme graduation, but with a different structure than Ruy's case:

**Phase 1 (LSI + CS#1-2): Interpersonal Effectiveness**
- The surface theme is "I can't give negative feedback / I make generic requests." This gets worked on through the Martin case. By CS#2, the surface problem is substantially handled -- Martin responded well, COACHEE2 now makes more specific requests.

**Phase 2 (CS#2-3): Motivation and Career Meaning**
- With the interpersonal surface resolved, a deeper theme emerges: What motivates me? What does career growth mean? This is identity-level reflection, but it emerges gently, almost intellectually. COACHEE2 reaches a genuine insight ("crecimiento es desarrollo de habilidades, no cambiar de puesto") but it is not connected to a coaching gap -- it is a philosophical shift.

**Phase 3 (CS#4-6): Procrastination (Self-Management)**
- The third theme is the deepest: procrastination as a self-management failure connected to motivation. The ITC framework reveals the big assumption. The behavioral test in CS#5-6 completes the arc.

**Unlike Ruy's case, the graduation here is horizontal rather than vertical.** Ruy's themes deepened from surface to root cause (missing conversations --> leadership identity). COACHEE2's themes shift laterally: interpersonal effectiveness --> motivation --> procrastination. They are related (procrastination connects to motivation, which connects to what one avoids doing with people), but the case does not reach a single unifying root cause. The coach notes this implicitly in the LSI debrief: "todavia veo que razona en un nivel muy instrumental, como si todo se tratara de un diseno de estrategias."

### Where Practice Diverges from Theory

**1. Contrato is implicit or absent throughout.**

Like Ruy's case, the formal three-objective contract (product, process, coaching) is never explicitly negotiated. In CASO 2, this is even more pronounced: CS#3 has no discernible contract at all. The session drifts through reflections without a clear coaching focus being agreed upon. This suggests that when the coachee arrives without a gap, the Contrato step is the one most at risk of being skipped -- and its absence affects the entire session's productivity.

**2. Role plays are absent.**

Despite COACHEE2's issues being explicitly conversational (giving feedback, making requests, handling non-compliance), Horacio never used role plays. In CS#1, the Martin conversation was ideal role-play material -- the coachee was about to have a difficult conversation. Instead, Horacio used conceptual teaching (Commitment Cycle) and anticipatory coaching. This is a notable divergence: the handbook presents role plays as the primary resolution tool, particularly for interpersonal challenges.

**3. Resolution quality varies enormously.**

CS#4's resolution (ITC + Rocio personal example) is described as the engagement's peak by the coachee himself: "por lejos la mejor sesion." The coach's personal self-disclosure (Rocio example) created identification and breakthrough. By contrast, CS#3's resolution is minimal -- a mindset shift and a tip. The variation suggests that resolution quality depends heavily on whether a genuine gap has been contracted.

**4. Coach's personal stories serve as resolution tools.**

Horacio shares his own experience with procrastination (the Rocio conversation) as an ITC example in CS#4. This is not prescribed in the handbook but produces the engagement's strongest moment. The coach's self-disclosure functions as a teaching story, an identification mirror, and a trust deepener simultaneously.

**5. RECORDATORIO notes function as Seguimiento.**

Horacio's personal reminders ("RECORDATORIO PARA CS#2: Tener a mano slides..."; "RECORDAR: tener esto en cuenta en el futuro") are the practical implementation of the handbook's Seguimiento step. They are coach-facing, not coachee-facing. There is no evidence of follow-up emails or between-session communication.

**6. The final session includes retrospective synthesis but no gift question.**

CS#6 includes a balance and retrospective, but lacks the "gift question" and "second season framing" observed in Ruy's case. The engagement closes "muy afablemente" but without a formal continuation mechanism. The coach's closing move -- revisiting the unaddressed feedback-reception topic -- is a probe, not a gift. It opens a thread rather than closing one.

## E. Praxis Principles Mapping

### 1. One Long Conversation

**PRESENT.**

The engagement reads as a continuous arc: LSI debrief seeds CS#1 (the coachee arrives already reflecting on the LSI gap). CS#1's Martin commitment feeds CS#2. CS#2's motivation thread carries through CS#3-4 and connects to procrastination. CS#5-6 close the procrastination arc. Horacio's RECORDATORIO notes are the connective tissue, and his ability to recap themes in CS#4 (when COACHEE2 asked) demonstrates the "one long conversation" in action.

However, this principle is **weaker than in Ruy's case.** The CS#3-CS#4 gap -- where no commitment carries forward and the coachee arrives without direction -- shows that "one long conversation" can lose momentum when the thread is not actively maintained through commitments.

### 2. Track What's Left Out

**PRESENT.**

Most clearly visible in the LSI debrief: the coach notes that COACHEE2's conclusion is "todavia... en un nivel muy instrumental, como si todo se tratara de un diseno de estrategias y no como la expresion autentica de un ser determinado." The coach notices what is missing (authenticity, BE-level work) even as the coachee declares his focus on relationship strategies.

Also visible in CS#6: Horacio revisits the feedback-reception topic that was "identified at the beginning of the process, and then never retomamos." He tracks what was parked and surfaces it before closing.

### 3. Yellow Lights

**PRESENT.**

Three explicit yellow lights in the notes:
1. **LSI Debrief:** "todavia veo que razona en un nivel muy instrumental" -- flagging instrumental reasoning as a pattern to watch.
2. **CS#2:** "RECORDAR: tener esto en cuenta en el futuro. COACHEE2 se muestra muy sensible al riesgo de que el otro se desmotive" -- flagging the sensitivity and luck attribution.
3. **CS#1:** "RECORDATORIO PARA CS#2: Tener a mano slides de ciclo del compromiso, y de cada herramienta // Repasar lo ocurrido con el compromiso."

These are interspersed in the session notes exactly as Horacio describes his practice.

### 4. Named Entity Tracking

**PRESENT.**

Martin is tracked from CS#1 through CS#2 (his trajectory, the feedback meeting, the performance improvement). Alberto is introduced in CS#2 as a motivation challenge case. Rocio appears in CS#4 as the coach's own named example. The notes track these people by name, enabling continuity.

However, named entities are used less richly than in the CASO 1 case study (Horacio's 6-session chemical company case with Julian, Maxi, Josafar). Here, Martin and Alberto appear and then drop, rather than weaving through the engagement.

### 5. Everything Reveals or Conceals

**PRESENT.**

Best evidence: the LSI debrief coach note about instrumental reasoning. COACHEE2 says he wants to revise his "estrategias de relacionamiento" -- the coach hears what this conceals: a lack of authentic self-work, treating relationships as engineering problems. "Hay en esta expresion algo que todavia veo que razona en un nivel muy instrumental."

Also: CS#1's observation that COACHEE2 "subordina/abandona sus propias ideas en beneficio de no desmotivar" -- the feedback-giving difficulty reveals a deeper pattern about self-abandonment.

### 6. Commitment as Doorway

**PRESENT (partially).**

CS#2 opens through the CS#1 commitment (Martin conversation). CS#6 opens through the CS#5 commitment (procrastination case). These are the engagement's two strongest openings after the LSI.

**Absent in CS#3 and CS#4,** which is precisely why those openings are weak. The principle is validated by its absence: when no commitment exists, the doorway into the next session is missing.

### 7. Outlier vs Design

**ABSENT.**

When COACHEE2 reports success with Martin in CS#2, Horacio does not ask "was this luck or design?" -- even though COACHEE2 himself attributes the result partly to "suerte." This is the exact situation where the Outlier vs Design reframe would be most relevant. Instead, Horacio flags the luck attribution as a yellow light but does not challenge it. This may be a deliberate choice: COACHEE2's sensitivity to "desmotivar" (which the coach notes) may have made the challenge feel too confrontational at that moment.

The absence is significant for AI Coach design: the Outlier vs Design intervention requires reading the coachee's emotional readiness, not just detecting the pattern.

### 8. Theme Graduation

**PRESENT (horizontal rather than vertical).**

Themes do graduate: interpersonal effectiveness (CS#1-2) resolves, making space for motivation (CS#2-3), which connects to procrastination (CS#4-6). But unlike Ruy's case, where graduation went from surface to root cause (vertical deepening), CASO 2's graduation is more lateral -- new themes emerge but they do not converge on a single root.

The coach's LSI debrief note about "instrumental reasoning" suggests a potential root cause that was never fully excavated: COACHEE2 treats everything as strategy rather than authentic self-expression. The procrastination work comes closest to the BE level, but the engagement ends without reaching a unified identity-level insight.

### 9. Side Comments as Signals

**PRESENT.**

CS#3: "En el ultimo tramo de la sesion, cuando parecia que COACHEE2 no tenia otros temas para plantear, me pregunto si yo tenia algo que sugerir." This side moment -- the coachee asking the coach for direction -- signals something: COACHEE2 may be running out of self-identified gaps, or may need the coach to name what the coachee cannot see.

CS#4: Procrastination emerged from a moment when COACHEE2 "expreso que una de las cosas que descubrio que quiere superar." The "discovery" happened in the middle of searching for a topic, not as a pre-formed agenda item. This is a side comment becoming the session's main theme.

### 10. Desdoblamiento

**ABSENT.**

No third-person self-observation exercise is described in any session. The closest approximation is CS#4's ITC analysis, which asks COACHEE2 to observe his own competing motivations -- but this is analytical, not the experiential "if you were observing yourself" technique used in Ruy's case.

### 11. Gift Question

**ABSENT.**

CS#6 closes "muy afablemente" but without a personalized self-coaching question left as a continuation tool. The coach instead probes the unaddressed feedback-reception topic -- opening a thread rather than gifting a question. The engagement ends without a portable self-coaching mechanism for COACHEE2.

### 12. Second Season Framing

**ABSENT.**

No framing of the engagement ending as a "chapter boundary." CS#6 is a closing balance, not a continuation invitation. This contrasts with Ruy's case where Horacio explicitly framed the end as a chapter, not a story ending.

### 13. Convergence in Final Session

**PRESENT.**

The coach adds a fourth probe (feedback reception). This is convergence -- the final session consolidates rather than expanding.

### New Praxis Principles Observed in CASO 2

**P19. Coach Self-Disclosure as Resolution Tool**

In CS#4, Horacio shares his own experience with a difficult conversation with Rocio as an ITC example. COACHEE2's reaction: "esta es por lejos la mejor sesion que tuvimos hasta ahora." The coach's personal story creates three simultaneous effects:
1. **Identification:** COACHEE2 sees himself in the coach's experience.
2. **Normalization:** If the coach has this pattern too, it is human, not defective.
3. **Concreteness:** The ITC framework becomes real through a specific example.

This is not in the handbook (which focuses on the coachee's material). It is not random self-disclosure -- it is a deliberately chosen teaching story that serves the resolution. The coach becomes the case study.

**P20. The Mid-Engagement Drift (and Recovery)**

CS#3-CS#4 represent a structural pattern not observed in the shorter Ruy engagement: a mid-engagement loss of momentum where the initial themes have been addressed but deeper work has not yet crystalized. The coachee arrives without a gap, the coach provides memory-based structure, and eventually a new theme emerges (procrastination in CS#4). This pattern may be inherent to 6-session engagements: the 3-session mark is where surface issues are consumed and the engagement must find its second wind.

The recovery mechanism is the coach's patience + the coachee's eventual self-discovery ("what I want to work on is procrastination"). The coach cannot force the deeper theme -- the coachee must find it. But the coach can hold the space and provide structural support (memory recap) until it emerges.

**P21. "Tarea" as Integration Homework (not just Action)**

In CS#4, the commitment is not a behavioral action (talk to X) but a preparatory/integrative task: "preparar su metodologia" considering ITC insights. This is a different type of commitment -- it asks the coachee to synthesize learning into a personal system. The outcome is not an external result but an internal framework. This type of commitment bridges the gap between behavioral (do X) and reflective (think about Y) commitments.

**P22. Coach's Note-to-Self as Structural Integrity Mechanism**

CASO 2 shows explicit RECORDATORIO and RECORDAR notes that function as the coach's private continuity system. These are not just yellow lights (topics to explore) but logistical/structural reminders: "tener a mano slides," "repasar lo ocurrido con el compromiso." They ensure that the coach arrives at the next session with both the materials and the follow-up questions needed to maintain the "one long conversation." For AI Coach design, these translate to pre-session preparation prompts.

## G. Key Observations for AI Coach Design

### 1. Commitment Gaps Create Structural Weakness -- AI Must Never Let a Session End Without One

CASO 2 demonstrates empirically that sessions without commitments (CS#2, CS#3) produce weaker openings in the following session. The AI Coach should implement a commitment extraction step as a required session-closing mechanism. When the coachee resists commitment (as in CS#5's "it's a process that takes time"), the AI must have escalating strategies: (a) reduce scope ("what's one small thing?"), (b) reframe the commitment type (reflective instead of behavioral), (c) name the resistance itself as material. Never allow a session to close without at least a minimal forward thread.

### 2. Mid-Engagement Drift is a Structural Pattern to Detect and Address

The CS#3-CS#4 drift -- where initial momentum is consumed and deeper themes have not yet emerged -- may be a predictable pattern in 6+ session engagements. The AI Coach should anticipate this around sessions 3-4 and have specific strategies: (a) provide a memory-based recap to help the coachee reconnect with the full arc, (b) surface yellow lights and parked topics that may now be ready for exploration, (c) offer the diagnostic data as a re-entry point ("your LSI showed X -- we haven't explored that yet"), (d) name the pattern ("it's normal for the initial topics to feel resolved at this point -- this is often when deeper patterns become visible").

### 3. Coach Self-Disclosure Has No AI Equivalent -- But Teaching Stories Can Be Pre-Loaded

CS#4's breakthrough came from the coach sharing a personal procrastination story (Rocio). The AI cannot share genuine personal experiences, but it can: (a) use anonymized case studies as teaching stories ("a leader I know of faced a similar pattern..."), (b) present framework examples concretely rather than abstractly, (c) use the coachee's own prior examples as mirrors ("this reminds me of when you described Martin -- you also initially avoided the direct conversation"). The principle is not self-disclosure per se but identification through concrete narrative.

### 4. Instrumental vs. Authentic Processing is a Coachee Typology the AI Must Detect

Horacio identifies early that COACHEE2 "razona en un nivel muy instrumental, como si todo se tratara de un diseno de estrategias." This is a coachee type: someone who approaches development as engineering rather than self-transformation. The AI Coach must detect this pattern (indicators: strategy language, optimization framing, avoidance of emotional content, treating relationships as systems to manage) and have a response: gently redirect toward the BE level, note the pattern as a yellow light, and -- when trust is sufficient -- name it directly ("I notice you often frame this as a strategy question -- what happens if we explore what you're actually feeling in that moment?").

### 5. The "Tuvo Suerte" Pattern Requires Careful Timing -- But Must Not Be Dropped

When a coachee attributes success to luck rather than competence, the "outlier vs design" challenge is critical for learning consolidation. In CASO 2, the coach chose not to deploy it due to the coachee's sensitivity. But the cost was real: the pattern was never addressed, and COACHEE2's self-efficacy regarding assertive conversations may remain fragile (built on "luck" rather than skill). The AI Coach should: (a) detect luck attribution, (b) flag it as a yellow light if immediate challenge is not appropriate, (c) revisit it in a later session when trust is stronger, and (d) never drop it entirely. Timing matters, but the intervention must happen eventually.
