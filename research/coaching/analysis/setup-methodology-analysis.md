## 1. Setup in Theory vs. Practice

### What Horacio Describes (Theory)

From `horacio-intervention-setups.md`, the standard pre-coaching setup includes:

1. **Reunion Cero (Meeting Zero)** — Coach, coachee, and coachee's boss meet together. Boss states expectations ("Espero que mejores tu capacidad de escucha"). These become reference points throughout the process.
2. **Diagnostic instrument** — LSI or CB Assessment administered before coaching begins. Horacio's strong opinion: "si le falta el diagnóstico individual, te estás perdiendo una enorme oportunidad de tocar la fibra de la conciencia de la persona."
3. **Mid-process touch-base** — Sponsor check-ins to ensure organizational alignment.
4. **Final tripartite meeting** — Coach, coachee, and boss review progress against initial objectives.

The theory implies a clean four-stage frame: Reunion Cero → Diagnostic → Sessions → Closing Tripartite.

### What the Cases Actually Show

| Element | Theory | CASO 1 | CASO 2 | CASO 3 | Ruy |
|---------|--------|--------|--------|--------|-----|
| **Reunion Cero (boss present)** | Required | Not documented | Not documented | Not documented | No — HR brokered only |
| **Boss expectations stated** | At Meeting Zero | Not documented | Not documented | Not documented | No boss present |
| **LSI administered** | Before sessions | Yes | Yes | Yes | No — already completed prior |
| **LSI debrief as standalone session** | Implied | Yes — full session (~2 hrs) | Yes — full session | Yes — full session | N/A |
| **Coach re-explains LSI from zero** | Not specified | Yes — "le expliqué el LSI completo desde 0" | Not explicit but detailed | Yes — "Dediqué bastante tiempo a explicarle la Red Sumarial" | N/A |
| **Coachee pre-reads report** | Not specified | Yes — but "interpretaciones bastante libres" | Yes — "me representa" | Not specified | N/A |
| **Mid-process sponsor check-in** | Yes | Not documented | Not documented | Not documented | N/A (4 sessions) |
| **Final tripartite meeting** | Yes | Not documented | Not documented | Not documented | No |
| **Duration** | Not specified | ~2 hours (coachee: "el tiempo pasó volando") | Not specified but substantial | Not specified but substantial | ~30 min |

### The Theory-Practice Gap

**The Reunion Cero is absent from all three case studies.** None of the cases document a boss being present at setup. The cases begin directly with the LSI debrief session. This doesn't necessarily mean Meeting Zero didn't happen — Horacio's case notes may start at the debrief because that's where coaching-relevant content begins. But the gap is notable: the theoretical framework emphasizes the tripartite setup, while the documented practice makes the LSI debrief the functional entry point.

**The LSI debrief has replaced Meeting Zero as the gateway session.** In all three corporate cases, the debrief is where the coaching relationship actually forms. It's where trust is built, awareness is seeded, and working themes emerge. Whatever happened organizationally before (selection, communication, diagnostic administration), the debrief is the moment the coachee becomes an active participant.

**The mid-process sponsor check-in and final tripartite meeting are also absent** from all case documentation. This may be a documentation gap (Horacio only wrote coaching-relevant content) or may reflect that these organizational ceremonies happen outside the coaching relationship proper.

## 2. The LSI Debrief as Gateway Session

### Cross-Case Anatomy

The LSI debrief in all three cases follows a consistent structure, though the specific content varies dramatically by coachee.

#### Phase 1: Biographical Anchoring

All three cases begin with extensive career and personal context — not as small talk, but as diagnostic material that the coach uses throughout the engagement.

**CASO 1:** "38 años de edad, 20 años en EMPRESA1. [...] Es Químico. Hizo carrera especialmente en Ventas, primero en Brasil. Tuvo experiencia internacional en Mexico entre el 2015-2018."

**CASO 2:** "Es Ingeniero Industrial. Gerente de ventas para la UN más grande [...] Hace 3 años en EMPRESA2 [...] Trabajó en Consultora de Packaging (la empresa se fue del país, pero él tuvo un proceso de coaching similar a éste en el outplacement de esa consultora. (tenía 24 años)"

**CASO 3:** "En EMPRESA3 desde hace 15 años. [...] Empezó apenas salido de la Universidad (becario). Estuvo en Customer Service (trainee – 4 meses). [...] En año 5, toma el Liderazgo del equipo de Customer Service. Empezó a tomar decisiones sobre personas (tenía 29 años). Me topé con la pared, aprendí 'a la brava'."

**Function:** The biographical context establishes (a) the coachee's career trajectory and identity, (b) previous leadership challenges and how they were handled, (c) prior experience with coaching/assessment instruments. In CASO 2, the prior coaching experience at age 24 is significant — it means COACHEE2 has a reference frame for the process. In CASO 3, the "a la brava" leadership origin story becomes relevant later when coaching addresses defensive leadership patterns.

#### Phase 2: Coachee Self-Identification of Development Areas

Before diving into LSI results, the coach elicits the coachee's own perception of where they need to grow. This creates a baseline that the LSI data can either confirm or challenge.

**CASO 1:** COACHEE1 "Se declaró muy contento y casi 'privilegiado' por tener la oportunidad." Also: "En el pasado le corrieron un DISC y quedó sorprendido por el grado de precisión. A partir de esa experiencia, les tiene mucha fe a estos instrumentos."

**CASO 3:** Explicit "Puntos a reforzar de mi liderazgo:" stated before any LSI discussion:
- "Ganar más firmeza en la toma de decisiones. Soy una persona muy conciliadora ('tú qué opinas?'). Hay veces que hay cosas que se tienen que hacer."
- "Organización de ideas: necesito reaccionar rápidamente, me cuesta dar forma a todo lo que tengo en la cabeza, me voy a lo urgente y no a lo importante."

**Function:** The pre-diagnostic self-assessment serves two purposes: (1) it gives the coach comparison material — where does the coachee's self-perception align with or diverge from the LSI data? (2) it creates a "before" snapshot that the coach can reference later when the coachee's understanding deepens.

#### Phase 3: Item-by-Item LSI Exploration

This is the longest and most substantive part of the debrief. The coach walks through LSI1 (self-perception) and LSI2 (others' perception), not as a data dump but as a guided conversation where each data point becomes a prompt for self-reflection.

**CASO 1 (Dependiente style — surprise and re-interpretation):**
> "LSI 1: Le sorprendió lo alto que salió Dependiente. No se siente así. Se declara una persona independiente. Declara que dice lo que piensa, que es muy directo."
>
> "LSI2: Sorpresa similar con el Dependiente. [...] Hizo una primera interpretación del item 'Dice lo que se espera' en el sentido de 'dice lo que piensa'. Le mostré que 'dice lo que piensa' podría entenderse como rojo más que como verde. Finalmente, hizo click cuando reconoció que en las juntas con sus colegas o jefes tiende a medir mucho sus palabras, ser políticamente correcto."

**CASO 1 (Perfeccionista — coach has difficulty):**
> "se enfrascó bastante en 'seeks recognition', que en LSI2 aparece con un puntaje cercano a 1. Me costó mostrarle una conexión entre ese enunciado y el 'Perfeccionismo', o incluso hacerle ver de qué manera ese enunciado podía entenderse como una oportunidad de mejora."

**CASO 2 (Bow tie pattern — coachee sees structural tension):**
> "Me llamó mucho la atención este efecto tirante entre 2 niveles muy altos. Bow tie: Competitivo – Aprobación. Reconoce la disyuntiva."

**CASO 2 (Coach plants seed — DO vs. BE gap):**
> "CONCLUSIÓN: Quiere revisar sus 'estrategias' de relacionamiento [...] [Nota: hay en esta expresión algo que todavía veo que razona en un nivel muy instrumental, como si todo se tratara de un diseño de estrategias y no como la expresión auténtica de un ser determinado]. Pero por otro lado también incluye en su conclusión que quiere hacer un trabajo 'interno'."
>
> "esta disociación entre lo que siente y hace en el momento, es reveladora de sus esfuerzos en el DO (=no reaccionar defensivamente), pero también de que en el nivel del BE (=qué piensa y siente en el momento) aún tiene un recorrido por delante. Le hablé de esto en la sesión."

**CASO 3 (Red Sumarial explanation — conceptual framework transfer):**
> "Dediqué bastante tiempo a explicarle la Red Sumarial, y mostrarle la importancia de que se enfoque en trabajar los verdes."

**Function:** The item-by-item walk is not data review — it's the first coaching intervention. Through guided exploration, the coachee begins making connections between abstract survey items and concrete behaviors. The coach's role is to correct misinterpretations, highlight patterns the coachee can't see, and plant seeds (yellow lights) for future sessions.

#### Phase 4: Coach's Private Framing

In each case, the coach concludes the debrief with private observations that set up the coaching direction. These are visible in Horacio's notes but were not necessarily communicated to the coachee.

**CASO 1:** Notes that COACHEE1 will work on Dependiente and Perfeccionista styles. No explicit coach concern noted — the coachee's engagement is high.

**CASO 2:** "[Nota: hay en esta expresión algo que todavía veo que razona en un nivel muy instrumental]" — coach privately flags that the coachee's framing is too strategic/DO-level and will need to shift to BE-level. This becomes a recurring theme.

**CASO 3:** "Será fundamental ayudarlo a salir del dilema binario entre hacer lo que el otro espera o hacer lo que yo quiero sin que el otro me importe." — coach identifies the core challenge before Session 1 even begins. This binary dilemma (green compliance vs. red assertion) becomes the thread of the entire engagement.

**Function:** The private framing is the coach's hypothesis about what the engagement will need to address. It's informed by the biographical context, the LSI data, and the coachee's reactions during the debrief. This framing guides yellow light placement and topic selection in early sessions.

#### Phase 5: Working Themes Emerge

By the end of the debrief, explicit working themes have been identified — sometimes by the coachee, sometimes by the coach.

**CASO 1:** Dependiente and Perfeccionista styles. Positive feedback, high engagement. "Trabajará sobre ambos estilos."

**CASO 2:** "Quiere revisar sus 'estrategias' de relacionamiento, porque encuentra que los impactos que quiere lograr no son los que está logrando." + internal work on accepting criticism.

**CASO 3:** Two explicit self-identified areas (firmeza, organización) + coach's private identification of the binary dilemma.

### Duration and Emotional Quality

**CASO 1:** Approximately 2 hours. "Su feedback final fue muy positivo (su mensaje: 'el tiempo pasó volando, pese a que estuvimos conversando casi 2 hs.')."

**CASO 2-3:** Duration not explicitly stated but the depth of content suggests similar extended sessions.

The debrief consistently produces positive emotional responses — coachees describe surprise, engagement, and the feeling that time passed quickly. This is significant: the debrief is the first trust-building moment of the engagement.

## 3. Case-by-Case Setup Extraction

### CASO 1: COACHEE1 / EMPRESA1

**Engagement context:** COACHEE1 is a 38-year-old chemical company sales leader with 20 years at EMPRESA1, managing 50 people across Brazil, Chile, and Argentina. Selected for coaching — "Se declaró muy contento y casi 'privilegiado' por tener la oportunidad de participar de este proceso, ya que en Brasil son casi 1500 personas pero sólo unas muy pocas están con este proceso de coaching."

**Pre-debrief preparation:**
- COACHEE1 had completed a DISC assessment previously and was positively predisposed to assessment instruments
- Had read the LSI report before the session, but interpretations were "bastante libres" (quite loose)
- Coach decided to re-explain LSI from zero: "le expliqué el LSI completo desde 0 ya que sus interpretaciones eran bastante 'libres'"

**Key debrief moments:**
- Surprise at high Dependiente score — self-concept as independent person challenged by data
- Difficulty with Perfeccionista / "seeks recognition" item — coach struggled to make the connection land
- Positive engagement throughout — coachee valued the space

**Coach's private observations:**
- No explicit yellow lights noted in the debrief, but the Dependiente surprise and the recognition difficulty both surface in later sessions
- The debrief established a pattern: COACHEE1 returns to LSI as "conversational re-entry" (Session 2: "Volvió entonces sobre el LSI y la sorpresa que le causó que el Dependiente saliera tan alto")

**Debrief → Session 1 transition:**
- Session 1 opens with a completely new topic (new organizational role, managing Ivan) — not a direct continuation of debrief themes
- The LSI becomes a background reference rather than a direct driver, resurfacing in Session 2 when COACHEE1 lacks a prepared topic

**What was NOT present:**
- No documented Reunion Cero
- No boss expectations stated
- No organizational goal alignment beyond being selected

### CASO 2: COACHEE2 / EMPRESA2

**Engagement context:** COACHEE2 is a 37-year-old industrial engineer and sales manager, 3 years at EMPRESA2, leading a team of 5. Previously had coaching during an outplacement at age 24 — brings prior coaching experience.

**Pre-debrief context gathering:**
- Extensive career trajectory elicitation: from industrial engineering to packaging to consultancy to EMPRESA2
- Prior coaching experience explicitly noted — this is not his first time with assessment instruments
- Career orientation choices documented ("Decidió orientar su carrera al área comercial")

**Key debrief moments:**
- Recognizes the "bow tie" tension between Competitivo and Aprobación: "Me llamó mucho la atención este efecto tirante entre 2 niveles muy altos"
- Coachee chooses to "double click" on specific items (Achievement, Dependiente, Oposición)
- Surprising finding: coachee doesn't realize others see his criticism as visible ("Le sorprendió que no lo ven criticando a otros a sus espaldas, aunque cree que sí lo hace")
- Accepts low trust tendency: "Aceptó que tiende a no confiar"

**Coach's private framing — the DO/BE gap (critical):**
- Coach privately notes the instrumental framing: "hay en esta expresión algo que todavía veo que razona en un nivel muy instrumental, como si todo se tratara de un diseño de estrategias"
- Explicitly identifies the DO/BE dissociation: coachee verbally accepts criticism but feels intense irritation internally. "Reveladora de sus esfuerzos en el DO (=no reaccionar defensivamente), pero también de que en el nivel del BE (=qué piensa y siente en el momento) aún tiene un recorrido por delante."
- This becomes a central thread: the gap between behavioral compliance and genuine integration

**Debrief → Session 1 transition:**
- Direct continuation: "COACHEE2 inició la sesión reflexionando sobre el 'error de comunicación hacia afuera' que revelaba la brecha entre LSI 1 y LSI 2"
- The LSI gap (self vs. others perception) becomes the Session 1 opening topic

**What was NOT present:**
- No documented Reunion Cero
- No boss expectations (though boss's difficult personality surfaces later as context)

### CASO 3: COACHEE3 / EMPRESA3

**Engagement context:** COACHEE3 is at EMPRESA3 for 15 years, started as a university intern, now manages LATAM supply chain planning. 5 direct reports, 7 countries, 6-7 plants.

**Pre-debrief context gathering — the most extensive biographical anchoring:**
- Full career history: university → intern → customer service → procurement → planning → LATAM leadership
- Key leadership crisis documented: "En año 5, toma el Liderazgo [...] Me topé con la pared, aprendí 'a la brava'. Primer acercamiento con el manejo del equipo."
- "En año 6, estaba anímicamente destrozado. Pedí un cambio" — emotional collapse and recovery documented

**Pre-diagnostic self-assessment (explicit):**
COACHEE3 states two development areas BEFORE looking at LSI:
1. "Ganar más firmeza en la toma de decisiones. Soy una persona muy conciliadora"
2. "Organización de ideas [...] me voy a lo urgente y no a lo importante"

**Key debrief moments:**
- LSI1: "Le hace sentido. Ve lógico la búsqueda de la Aprobación"
- Boss feedback integrated: "Mi jefe me ha dicho que soy muy negativo, aunque me dijo que también le sirve"
- LSI2: "Sorprendido por la diferencia de percepción entre otros y él mismo"
- Coach explicitly teaches the Red Sumarial framework

**Coach's private framing — the binary dilemma (critical):**
> "Será fundamental ayudarlo a salir del dilema binario entre hacer lo que el otro espera o hacer lo que yo quiero sin que el otro me importe."

This single sentence frames the entire 6-session engagement. The binary dilemma (green compliance vs. red assertion) is the coach's diagnostic hypothesis, formulated during the debrief, that guides all subsequent interventions including the role play in Session 4.

**Debrief → Session 1 transition:**
- COACHEE3 analyzes the report again independently between sessions: "Analicé el reporte nuevamente. Busqué oportunidades nuevamente"
- Session 1 opens with coachee's own analysis: "Lo que identifiqué es que me reconozco en un liderazgo defensivo"
- Two self-identified themes plus a new one (crisis management/apagafuegos)

**What was NOT present:**
- No documented Reunion Cero
- Boss mentioned as context (gives feedback about negativity) but not present in setup

## 4. Ruy's Setup (Comparison)

Ruy's December 17, 2025 setup is a special case that deviates from the standard LSI-based setup in every structural dimension while preserving several core functions.

### Structural Differences

| Dimension | LSI-Based Cases (1-3) | Ruy's Setup |
|-----------|----------------------|-------------|
| **Gateway instrument** | LSI (standardized, quantitative) | None — Ruy had already completed LSI and CB Assessment |
| **Duration** | ~2 hours | ~30 minutes |
| **Biographical anchoring** | Extensive career history | Minimal — pre-existing relationship |
| **Coach preparation** | Read LSI results, prepared framework explanations | Knew Ruy's context from prior working relationship |
| **Boss/sponsor present** | Not documented (but organizationally framed) | No — coachee-initiated, HR brokered |
| **Goal identification method** | LSI data → surprise → exploration → themes | Open question → gap list co-creation → magic wand technique |
| **Number of working themes** | 2-3 (LSI-derived) | 8 (conversation-derived) |
| **Private framing** | Coach formulates diagnostic hypothesis | Not documented (shorter session, less data) |
| **Process explanation** | Full LSI re-explanation from zero | Light — existing relationship reduced need |
| **Homework** | Implicit (coachee may re-read report) | None |

### Functional Equivalences

Despite these structural differences, both approaches achieve the same core functions:

1. **Trust establishment** — In the cases, the LSI debrief builds trust through deep engagement with personal data. For Ruy, the pre-existing relationship provided the trust base.

2. **Theme identification** — The cases use LSI as a structured tool to surface themes (surprise → exploration → commitment). Ruy's setup uses open questioning and the magic wand technique to surface themes directly from lived experience.

3. **Coach's mental model** — In the cases, the debrief gives the coach a rich mental model of the coachee (career history, self-perception, LSI patterns, emotional reactions). Ruy's existing relationship provided this context, but the gap list created a structured reference for the coaching arc.

4. **Raw material for Session 1** — In all cases (including Ruy), the setup creates the "conversational pantry" that Session 1 draws from. For the cases, it's the LSI items and coachee's reactions. For Ruy, it's the 8-item gap list.

### What Ruy's Case Uniquely Reveals

The magic wand technique is not present in any of the three case studies. It appears to be a tool Horacio deploys when:
- There is no structured assessment data to drive the conversation
- The coachee has difficulty articulating gaps
- The presenting desire is vague ("I want the commercial team to work" — but what's the gap?)

This technique converts vague desires into articulable gaps by offering an ideal scenario prompt: "If you could have anything, what would it look like?" The fact that it doesn't appear in the LSI cases suggests the LSI data itself performs this function — the instrument surfaces gaps that the coachee couldn't articulate on their own.

## 5. Setup Praxis Principles

These principles are specific to the pre-coaching/setup phase. They complement the 30 inter-session praxis principles documented in `coaching-cases-analysis.md`.

### SP-1: Biographical Anchoring as Diagnostic Material

**Pattern:** The coach elicits extensive career and personal history at the start of the debrief — not as rapport-building small talk but as diagnostic data that informs the entire engagement.

**Evidence:**
- CASO 1: Career trajectory (chemistry → sales → international → regional leadership) explains the position-sensitivity visible in Dependiente scores
- CASO 2: Prior coaching experience at 24 means coachee has a reference frame — coach can calibrate accordingly
- CASO 3: "Me topé con la pared, aprendí 'a la brava'" — the traumatic first leadership experience explains the defensive patterns visible in the LSI

**Design implication:** The AI Coach needs a structured biographical intake that goes beyond demographics. Career history, leadership experiences, prior coaching/assessment experience, and self-identified development areas should be captured before any assessment debrief.

### SP-2: Pre-Diagnostic Self-Assessment

**Pattern:** Before or during the debrief, the coachee articulates their own perception of development needs. The coach then uses the LSI data to confirm, challenge, or deepen this self-assessment.

**Evidence:**
- CASO 1: COACHEE1 expects to see independence confirmed — surprised by Dependiente score. The gap between self-perception and data creates a productive tension.
- CASO 3: COACHEE3 explicitly lists two development areas ("firmeza en la toma de decisiones" and "organización de ideas") before seeing LSI results. The LSI confirms and deepens these.

**Design implication:** The AI Coach should capture the user's self-assessment BEFORE presenting any diagnostic results. This creates a compare-and-contrast moment that drives awareness.

### SP-3: Coach Re-Explains Instrument from Zero

**Pattern:** Even when the coachee has pre-read their report, the coach re-explains the instrument from scratch. This isn't redundancy — it's quality control over interpretation.

**Evidence:**
- CASO 1: "le expliqué el LSI completo desde 0 ya que sus interpretaciones eran bastante 'libres'"
- CASO 3: "Dediqué bastante tiempo a explicarle la Red Sumarial"

**Design implication:** The AI Coach assessment debrief must include a full explanation of the instrument, not just results. Users who self-interpret may misunderstand items (CASO 1's "Dice lo que se espera" misread as "Dice lo que piensa").

### SP-4: Item-Level Guided Exploration

**Pattern:** The debrief walks through individual items, not just aggregate scores. Specific items become conversation starters where the coachee connects abstract survey language to concrete behaviors.

**Evidence:**
- CASO 1: "Hizo una primera interpretación del item 'Dice lo que se espera' en el sentido de 'dice lo que piensa'. Le mostré que 'dice lo que piensa' podría entenderse como rojo más que como verde. Finalmente, hizo click cuando reconoció que en las juntas con sus colegas o jefes tiende a medir mucho sus palabras."
- CASO 2: Coachee "Eligió hacer Doble click en:" specific items — the exploration is collaborative, not one-directional.
- CASO 2: Coach highlights a "perlita" — "Reacts rather than initiates [...] lo destaqué yo como 'perlita' porque encabeza la lista, tiene un puntaje cercano a 1"

**Design implication:** The AI assessment debrief should not present scores in bulk. It should walk through items sequentially, asking the user to interpret each one and offering alternative readings when needed. The "double click" mechanism (letting the user choose which items to explore deeper) should be implemented.

### SP-5: Coach's Private Diagnostic Hypothesis

**Pattern:** During the debrief, the coach formulates a private diagnostic hypothesis — a framing of the core challenge that will guide the coaching arc. This is not shared with the coachee but shapes topic selection, yellow light placement, and intervention timing.

**Evidence:**
- CASO 2: "[Nota: hay en esta expresión algo que todavía veo que razona en un nivel muy instrumental]" — the coach identifies the DO/BE gap as the core issue
- CASO 3: "Será fundamental ayudarlo a salir del dilema binario entre hacer lo que el otro espera o hacer lo que yo quiero sin que el otro me importe" — the binary dilemma becomes the organizing frame

**Design implication:** The AI Coach needs an internal "hypothesis" field in its memory architecture — a coach-side interpretation of the coachee's core challenge that evolves over sessions. This is distinct from the coachee-visible goal and may differ from what the coachee articulates as their need.

### SP-6: LSI as Conversational Pantry Seed

**Pattern:** The debrief creates a rich store of specific items, reactions, surprises, and commitments that serve as raw material for future sessions. The LSI is not consumed in the debrief — it's planted as a "conversational pantry" that the coach and coachee return to.

**Evidence:**
- CASO 1, Session 2: "COACHEE1 [...] Volvió entonces sobre el LSI y la sorpresa que le causó que el Dependiente saliera tan alto" — when coachee lacks a prepared topic, the LSI becomes the default re-entry point
- CASO 2, Session 1: "inició la sesión reflexionando sobre el 'error de comunicación hacia afuera' que revelaba la brecha entre LSI 1 y LSI 2" — LSI gap directly opens Session 1
- CASO 3, Session 1: "Analicé el reporte nuevamente. Busqué oportunidades nuevamente" — coachee independently re-processed the LSI between sessions

**Design implication:** This principle was already identified in the inter-session analysis as "Diagnostic as Conversational Re-entry" (P19). The setup analysis confirms that the seed is planted deliberately during the debrief — the coach ensures enough items are explored to create a rich pantry, and deliberately leaves some items unresolved to motivate return.

### SP-7: Surprise as Engagement Driver

**Pattern:** The most productive debrief moments occur when the data surprises the coachee — when their self-perception is challenged by the instrument. These surprise moments create the emotional engagement that makes coaching stick.

**Evidence:**
- CASO 1: "Le sorprendió lo alto que salió Dependiente. No se siente así." — this surprise drives the entire Dependiente exploration
- CASO 2: "Le sorprendió que no lo ven criticando a otros a sus espaldas, aunque cree que sí lo hace" — the gap between self-perception and others' perception creates awareness
- CASO 3: "Sorprendido por la diferencia de percepción entre otros y él mismo"

**Design implication:** The AI Coach assessment debrief should be designed to maximize productive surprise. Present areas of largest self-other gap first. When a user shows surprise, pause and explore rather than moving to the next item.

### SP-8: Commitment Framing in Debrief

**Pattern:** The debrief ends with some form of commitment or working direction, even though it's not a coaching session per se. The coachee leaves with a declared intention.

**Evidence:**
- CASO 1: "Trabajará sobre ambos estilos [Dependiente and Perfeccionista]"
- CASO 2: "Quiere revisar sus 'estrategias' de relacionamiento" + wants to do "internal work"
- CASO 3: Two self-identified areas + prioritization ("Trabajaría en los estilos 11 y 12. Y bajar el verde")

**Design implication:** The AI Coach pre-coaching flow should close with the user identifying 2-3 focus areas. These become the initial "conversational pantry" items for Session 1.

### SP-9: Magic Wand Technique (Non-LSI Setup)

**Pattern:** When no structured assessment data is available, the coach uses ideal-scenario questioning ("If you could have anything, what would it look like?") to convert vague desires into articulable gaps.

**Evidence:**
- Ruy's setup: Used when Ruy struggled to articulate certain gaps. "Some gaps were not immediately clear to Ruy (e.g., 'the commercial team just works' — hard to articulate what's missing when the presenting feeling is just 'I want this')"

**Design implication:** The AI Coach should have a fallback "magic wand" technique for when structured assessment data is unavailable or insufficient. This is particularly relevant for users who enter coaching without completing a formal assessment.

### SP-10: Coach Preparation Before Debrief

**Pattern:** The coach reviews the coachee's background and LSI results before the debrief session, forming preliminary observations. The debrief is not an improvised reading of the data — the coach arrives with a prepared framework.

**Evidence:**
- CASO 1: Coach had already decided to re-explain LSI from zero based on pre-reading
- CASO 2: Coach already identified the "perlita" (Dependiente item) to highlight
- CASO 3: Coach already prepared to focus on the Red Sumarial

**Design implication:** The AI Coach equivalent is pre-processing assessment data to identify: (a) largest gaps between self and others, (b) items most likely to surprise, (c) preliminary diagnostic hypothesis. This processing should happen before the debrief conversation begins.

## 6. AI Coach Design Implications

### 6.1 Pre-Coaching Flow Architecture

Based on the analysis, the AI Coach pre-coaching flow should include these stages:

```
1. BIOGRAPHICAL INTAKE
   - Career trajectory, current role, team size, reporting structure
   - Previous coaching/assessment experience
   - How the user arrived at coaching (self-initiated? recommended?)

2. SELF-ASSESSMENT CAPTURE
   - "Before we look at any data, where do you think your development areas are?"
   - Open-ended + structured (e.g., select from categories)
   - This creates the compare-and-contrast baseline

3. ASSESSMENT ADMINISTRATION
   - CB Assessment (or LSI equivalent)
   - Completed before debrief session

4. AI PRE-PROCESSING (internal)
   - Identify largest self-other gaps
   - Flag items likely to produce surprise
   - Generate preliminary diagnostic hypothesis
   - Design debrief conversation flow

5. GUIDED DEBRIEF SESSION
   - Full instrument explanation
   - Item-by-item exploration (coach-led, with user "double click" choice points)
   - Surprise exploration (pause and deepen when gap is found)
   - Red Sumarial / framework teaching
   - Working theme identification (2-3 areas)
   - Commitment framing

6. INTERNAL COACHING SETUP (invisible to user)
   - Store diagnostic hypothesis
   - Populate conversational pantry with unresolved items
   - Set yellow lights for future sessions
   - Create Session 1 opening plan
```

### 6.2 Key Design Decisions

**Should the AI Coach have a "Meeting Zero" with a boss/sponsor?**
The case data suggests this is organizationally valuable but practically rare even in human coaching. For the AI Coach MVP: no. The coaching relationship starts at the individual level. Organizational alignment can be added later for enterprise deployments.

**How long should the AI debrief be?**
Human debriefs run ~2 hours. The AI Coach should plan for a substantial session (not a 5-minute summary). The debrief is where the coaching relationship is seeded — rushing it undermines everything downstream. Consider breaking it into 2-3 shorter interactions if attention span is a concern.

**What replaces the biographical anchoring?**
The human coach listens to career history and extracts diagnostic signals in real-time. The AI Coach can (a) capture this data through a structured intake form, (b) ask follow-up questions that probe for the diagnostic signals (leadership crises, career transitions, relationship with authority), (c) integrate this data with assessment results during pre-processing.

**What replaces the coach's private diagnostic hypothesis?**
This is a core memory architecture question. The AI Coach needs an internal "coaching lens" field — updated after each interaction — that captures the AI's interpretation of the coachee's core challenge. This lens should influence question selection, topic surfacing, and intervention timing, but never be shared directly with the user.

### 6.3 What the AI Coach Can Do Better

1. **Perfect recall of debrief items** — The human coach may forget specific items from a 2-hour debrief. The AI remembers every reaction, every surprise, every unresolved item.
2. **Consistent re-explanation** — The AI can always explain the instrument thoroughly. No variability in quality of framework teaching.
3. **Pre-processing at scale** — The AI can analyze assessment results before the conversation, identifying every significant gap and surprise point, and designing the conversation flow accordingly.
4. **Conversational pantry management** — The AI can systematically track which items were explored, which were deferred, and which produced the strongest reactions, then surface these optimally in future sessions.

### 6.4 What the AI Coach Cannot Replicate

1. **Reading the room during item exploration** — The human coach knows when to push ("Me costó mostrarle una conexión") and when to back off. The AI lacks real-time emotional calibration.
2. **Biographical signal extraction** — The human coach hears "aprendí a la brava" and immediately connects it to defensive leadership patterns. The AI needs explicit rules or training to make these connections.
3. **Private concern as art** — The way Horacio formulates "Será fundamental ayudarlo a salir del dilema binario..." is a masterful diagnostic compression. The AI's diagnostic hypothesis will be more formulaic.

## 7. Refined Phase C Questions

Based on this analysis, the Phase C Horacio interview questions should be sharpened:

### Setup Process Questions

1. **Reunion Cero in practice:** "In the three case studies, the Reunion Cero doesn't appear. Does it always happen? When it does, what specifically happens? What's the conversation structure? How long does it take?"

2. **The debrief as gateway:** "The cases suggest the LSI debrief is where the coaching relationship really begins. Is this intentional? Is the debrief designed as the first coaching intervention, or is it purely informational?"

3. **Coach preparation ritual for debriefs:** "What do you do before an LSI debrief? How much time do you spend with the results? What are you looking for when you pre-read?"

4. **Item-level strategy:** "In CASO 1, you re-explained the LSI from zero because interpretations were 'bastante libres.' Is this standard practice? How do you decide which items to explore in detail vs. which to skip?"

5. **The 'perlita' technique:** "In CASO 2, you highlighted a specific item as a 'perlita.' How do you select which items to highlight proactively vs. waiting for the coachee to notice them?"

### Diagnostic Hypothesis Questions

6. **Private framing:** "In CASO 2 you noted the coachee was thinking 'instrumentally' about behavior change. In CASO 3 you identified the 'binary dilemma.' When in the process do you form these hypotheses? Do they usually prove correct? How do they influence your session planning?"

7. **Theory of the case:** "When you finish the debrief, do you have a 'theory of the case' — a hypothesis about what the core challenge will turn out to be? How often does this evolve vs. stay stable?"

### Transition Questions

8. **Debrief → Session 1 bridge:** "In CASO 1, Session 1 opened with a completely new topic (not LSI-related). In CASO 2, Session 1 directly continued the LSI gap. Is this variance typical? Do you guide the transition or let the coachee drive it?"

9. **Homework between debrief and Session 1:** "Do you assign anything between the debrief and Session 1? (CASO 3's coachee re-analyzed the report independently — was this encouraged?)"

### Ruy-Specific Validation

10. **Non-LSI setup:** "With Ruy, you didn't use the LSI. The gap list replaced the structured assessment. Is this approach you use with other coachees who've already completed diagnostics, or was it specific to the context?"

11. **Magic wand technique:** "You used ideal-scenario questioning with Ruy to help articulate gaps. Is this a technique you use frequently? When do you deploy it vs. more direct questioning?"
