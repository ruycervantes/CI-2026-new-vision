### 3. Gap List Creation

Horacio asked Ruy where the gaps were — where things needed help. Through conversation, they co-created a list of **8 topics**. Horacio wrote these down during the session and later gave the written list to Ruy. The notes were filed under "Orientación del Desarrollo de Features, Equipos Comercios."

**The 8 gaps (Ruy's words, from Horacio's notes):**

1. **Este equipo comercial funciona** — The commercial team just works, it just works well
2. **Conectar comercial con producto** — Connect commercial with product
3. **Hacer lo necesario para que el producto funcione** — Do what's needed to make the product work
4. **Más usuarios para recibir más feedback sobre mi producto** — More users to get more product feedback
5. **Más tiempo para platicar con el equipo y rebotar ideas** — More time to talk with the team and bounce ideas
6. **Continuar** — Continue (momentum/persistence)
7. **Contar con una oficina para poder ir de manera presencial** — Have an office to be there in person
8. **Conversaciones estratégicas efectivas. ¿Cómo sabemos que lo que estamos haciendo es lo adecuado?** — Effective strategic conversations. How do we know what we're doing is right?

**Elicitation technique — "Magic Wand" questions:**
- Some gaps were not immediately clear to Ruy (e.g., "the commercial team just works" — hard to articulate what's missing when the presenting feeling is just "I want this")
- Horacio used **magic wand / ideal scenario questions** to surface the underlying gap: "If you could have anything, what would it look like?"
- Example: the office topic (#7) — being remote was the reality, but the magic wand question surfaced that Ruy wanted physical presence with the team
- This technique helped convert vague desires into concrete gap statements

**Observations:**
- Topics span operational (product, users), relational (team time, commercial-product connection), and strategic (how do we know we're on track)
- They are interconnected — not a flat list but a web of related concerns
- Topic #8 (strategic conversations) became the primary focus in Session 1 — the most abstract and identity-level gap on the list
- During the session, Horacio also began working on understanding what the gaps meant — the setup was partly exploratory, not purely logistical

## Connection to Session 1

The gap list created in this setup became the raw material for Session 1's opening. From the Session 1 analysis:

> Horacio opened by reading back items from a prior conversation — the setup session list. This "reconnect" move (reading back the coachee's own words) serves as both memory signal and structured choice: "Which of these do you want to work on?"

Ruy chose topic #8 (strategic conversations) — the most abstract and identity-level item on the list. The other 7 topics were tracked but deferred, visible in the "Track What's Left Out" praxis principle. Over Sessions 1-4, the operational items (product, users, office) largely fell away as the coaching deepened into leadership identity — a pattern consistent with the Theme Graduation principle.

## Methodological Finding: Magic Wand as Setup Technique

The "magic wand" elicitation technique is a new praxis observation not previously documented. It serves a specific function in the setup: **converting vague desires into articulable gaps.**

When the coachee knows something is missing but can't name it (e.g., "the commercial team just works" — what's the gap?), the coach offers an ideal scenario prompt to surface the underlying need. This is related to but distinct from the "Outlier vs Design" principle (which operates during sessions on reported behavior). The magic wand operates during setup on presenting concerns.

**For AI Coach design:** This technique could be implemented in the pre-coaching flow — when a user struggles to articulate their development goals, use ideal-scenario prompting to help them surface gaps.
