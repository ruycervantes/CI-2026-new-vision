### What Makes a Principle Universal?

The 11 universal principles share a common trait: **they are structural, not technique-dependent.** They describe the architecture of how coaching works across sessions (One Long Conversation, Theme Graduation, Commitment as Doorway) or the coach's listening/tracking stance (Yellow Lights, Named Entity Tracking, Track What's Left Out).

The 5 context-dependent principles share a different trait: **they are specific interventions.** Gift Question, Desdoblamiento, Outlier vs Design, and Frustration-as-Signal Reframe are techniques deployed based on coachee readiness and session context.

**For AI Coach design:** The 11 universal principles must be built into the system architecture. The 5 context-dependent techniques should be available in the AI's repertoire but deployed based on contextual conditions (trust level, coachee type, session position).

## 3. New Praxis Principles Discovered

Across the three new cases, 12 new praxis principles were identified (with overlap across cases). After deduplication and synthesis:

### Consolidated New Principles

| # | Principle | First Observed | Also In | AI Coach Design Implication |
|---|-----------|---------------|---------|----------------------------|
| **19** | **Diagnostic as Conversational Re-entry Point** | CASO 1 (CS#2) | CASO 2 (CS#6), CASO 3 (CS#1) | The LSI is not a one-time input — it's a persistent "conversational pantry." When the coachee has no topic, returning to unexplored diagnostic items opens new threads. The AI should track which diagnostic items have been explored and which remain unopened. |
| **20** | **The Vacuum as Productive Space** | CASO 1 (CS#2, CS#4) | CASO 3 (CS#3 "bache") | When the coachee arrives "empty" or a session hits a dead spot, this is not a failure — it may signal readiness for deeper work. The AI must resist filling silence and instead create space for what emerges. |
| **21** | **Parallel Track Recognition** | CASO 1 (coach's meta-observation) | — | The coach recognizes that the engagement has two separate developmental tracks that don't converge. This meta-structural awareness is important for AI design: the AI should monitor whether themes are converging toward a root cause or running in parallel. |
| **22** | **The Safe Space as Primary Value** | CASO 1 (CS#1-3, CS#6) | — | Some coachees' primary value from coaching is having someone to talk to. The relational container is therapeutic. The AI must distinguish between "feels heard" (necessary but insufficient) and "actually developing" (the goal). |
| **23** | **Behavioral Substitution Pattern** | CASO 1 (CS#4) | — | The coachee does "something productive" (behavioral changes) that sidesteps harder internal work (cognitive/identity). Distinct from avoidance (doing nothing). The AI should track the TYPE of commitment (behavioral, cognitive, identity) and flag mismatches between what was offered and what was done. |
| **24** | **Mirror Cases Across the Arc** | CASO 1 (Ivan → Ignacio) | — | Structurally similar situations recurring in the engagement. The second instance benefits from the first. The AI should detect parallels and surface them: "This reminds me of X — what's similar? What's different?" |
| **25** | **Coach Self-Disclosure as Resolution Tool** | CASO 2 (CS#4, Rocio story) | — | Deliberate sharing of the coach's own experience produces identification, normalization, and concreteness. The AI cannot self-disclose but can use anonymized case studies and the coachee's own prior examples as mirrors. |
| **26** | **Mid-Engagement Drift (and Recovery)** | CASO 2 (CS#3-CS#4) | CASO 1 (CS#4) | Around sessions 3-4, initial momentum is consumed and deeper work hasn't crystallized. This is a predictable pattern in 6-session engagements. The AI should anticipate this, provide memory-based recaps, and surface parked yellow lights. |
| **27** | **"Tarea" as Integration Homework** | CASO 2 (CS#4) | — | Commitments that ask the coachee to synthesize learning into a personal system (not just take an action). A bridge between behavioral and reflective commitments. |
| **28** | **Role Play as Modeling (Not Just Practice)** | CASO 3 (CS#4) | — | The coach demonstrates a behavior the coachee has never performed (behavioral template transfer). Distinct from practice role play. This is a natural AI strength — generating contextually grounded examples using the coachee's actual entities and situations. |
| **29** | **Acceptance as Resolution** | CASO 3 (CS#2) | — | The brecha closes not because the situation changed but because the coachee's stance shifted. The AI must recognize conscious acceptance as a valid resolution type, not always push for action. |
| **30** | **Micro-Escalation in Skill Teaching** | CASO 3 (CS#4→CS#5) | — | Full demonstration first, then targeted micro-practice. The AI should track which skills have been introduced vs. which are being refined and adapt the teaching format accordingly. |

### The "Session 3 Emergence" Pattern

One of the most striking cross-case findings: in 3 of 4 cases, the deeper coaching theme emerges around Session 3, often from a side comment or an unexpected moment.

| Case | What Emerged | How |
|------|-------------|-----|
| **Ruy** | Leadership identity (not owning the role) | Side comment in Session 3 that Horacio caught as signal |
| **CASO 1** | Family pattern behind catastrophic thinking | Deep exploration in CS#3 (but then stalled) |
| **CASO 2** | Procrastination as self-management challenge | CS#4 (after CS#3's drift — one session later than the pattern) |
| **CASO 3** | COLEGA1/assertiveness challenge | Final minutes of CS#3, after a "bache," almost as an afterthought |

**Pattern:** Surface issues require ~2-3 sessions to handle. Once the operational/tactical layer is addressed, psychological space opens for the deeper material. The emergence often happens laterally (side comment, dead spot, unplanned topic) rather than through direct questioning.

**AI Coach implication:** The AI should anticipate that sessions 3-4 are the transition zone where deeper material surfaces. It should be prepared to catch emergent themes, tolerate lower-energy sessions, and resist the urge to force productivity.

### Commitment Consistency and Opening Quality

| Case | Sessions with commitment | Sessions without | Correlation with opening quality |
|------|------------------------|-----------------|----------------------------------|
| **Ruy** | 4/4 (100%) | 0 | All openings strong — commitment follow-up provides entry point |
| **CASO 1** | 4/6 (67%) | CS#2, CS#6 | CS#3 and CS#4 (following no-commitment sessions) have weakest openings |
| **CASO 2** | 4/6 (67%) | CS#2, CS#3 | CS#3 and CS#4 are the drift sessions — directly caused by missing commitments |
| **CASO 3** | 5/6 (83%) | CS#6 (closing) | All openings except CS#2 (no coachee agenda) have threads; CS#2 rescued by coach's prepared follow-up |

**Finding:** There is a strong correlation between commitment consistency and session opening quality. Sessions following a commitment-free session are weaker. The AI Coach must implement commitment extraction as a non-optional session-closing step.

### Resolution Depth Comparison

| Case | Deepest resolution reached | Resolution technique | Resolution level |
|------|--------------------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| **Ruy** | Identity declaration ("I accept, like a marriage") | Direct acceptance question, convergence synthesis | BE (identity) |
| **CASO 1** | Behavioral discipline (diet, email cutoff) | Cognitive tools offered but not engaged; behavioral substitution | DO (behavioral) |
| **CASO 2** | ITC framework internalized ("adelantar el tiempo del sufrimiento") | Coach self-disclosure + ITC framework + behavioral test | DO→BE transition |
| **CASO 3** | Reclamo skill learned but green pattern reasserts | Role play modeling + micro-practice + truth-telling | DO (skill), BE unresolved |

**Finding:** Only Ruy's case reaches full identity-level resolution. The 6-session cases show more realistic patterns: skills are learned, behaviors change, but the deep identity work often remains incomplete. This is not failure — it is the normal range of coaching outcomes.

**AI Coach implication:** The AI should track resolution depth and not equate "the coachee reports satisfaction" with "the root cause has been addressed." CASO 1's COACHEE1 reported satisfaction while avoiding cognitive work. CASO 3's COACHEE3 reported "tranquilidad" while the green pattern reasserted.

### Role Play Usage

| Case | Role Play Used? | Context | Technique |
|------|:-:|---------|-----------|
| **Ruy** | No | Issues were organizational/identity — no specific conversation to rehearse | N/A |
| **CASO 1** | No | Two clear opportunities (Ivan CS#1, Ignacio CS#5) were not taken | N/A — notable absence |
| **CASO 2** | No | Conversational issues (feedback, requests) could have used role play | N/A — notable absence |
| **CASO 3** | **Yes** | COLEGA1 reclamo — coachee had literally never performed the behavior | Modified Direct (coach models) + micro-role plays |

**Finding:** Role play was used in only 1 of 4 cases, and only when the coachee needed to learn a behavior they had never performed. The handbook describes role play as the primary resolution tool, but in practice it is rare and context-specific. When used, Horacio adapted the technique (Modified Direct Version, not the standard handbook form).

**AI Coach implication:** Role play modeling (the coach demonstrating the behavior using the coachee's context) is a natural AI strength and should be available as a resolution tool, but should not be the default.

### Coach's Role Across the Arc

| Session Position | Ruy | CASO 1 | CASO 2 | CASO 3 |
|-----------------|-----|--------|--------|--------|
| **Opening (LSI/Setup)** | Navigator | Teacher/interpreter | Diagnostician | Diagnostician/educator |
| **Early (S1-2)** | Architect's assistant | Listener/facilitator | Guide/framework provider | Navigator/accountant |
| **Mid (S2-3)** | Depth explorer | Diagnostic guide/depth explorer | Patient listener | Space-holder/catcher |
| **Late (S4-5)** | Synthesizer | Challenger/metaphor maker | Teacher/storyteller → Challenger | Modeler/skill teacher |
| **Closing (S4/CS#6)** | Synthesizer + gift giver | Teacher (Aprendiz model) | Synthesizer | Truth-teller/closer |

**Finding:** The coach's role is not fixed — it adapts to what the coachee needs at each session position. Common pattern: Diagnostician/Navigator → Explorer → Teacher/Challenger → Synthesizer. But the specific role within each position varies by coachee type.

## 5. Coachee Typology Observations

### Four Coachee Types Observed

| Type | Case | Characteristics | What Works | What Doesn't |
|------|------|----------------|-----------|-------------|
| **Identity Seeker** | Ruy | Self-directed, high metacognitive capacity, brings rich material, responds to challenge | Direct questioning, desdoblamiento, gift question, convergence synthesis | Would resist role play (too performative for the issue type) |
| **Safe Space User** | CASO 1 | Values the coaching container itself, substitutes behavioral work for cognitive work, consistent appreciation | Patience, safe space provision, long-term relationship, metaphors | Cognitive deconstruction (offered but not engaged), forced depth work |
| **Instrumental Reasoner** | CASO 2 | Approaches development as engineering, treats relationships as systems, strong on execution | Frameworks (ITC, Daniel Pink), concrete examples, coach self-disclosure | Abstract self-work, sessions without concrete goals |
| **Defensive Practitioner** | CASO 3 | Long-tenured, conflict-avoidant, capable but passive, internalizes accountability structure over time | Role play/modeling, explicit skill teaching, naming patterns directly | Silence-only approaches (needs prompting initially), abstract frameworks without practice |

**For AI Coach design:** The AI should detect coachee type early (through diagnostic data + first 2-3 session patterns) and adapt its intervention repertoire accordingly. This is not a fixed categorization — coachees may shift types as the engagement progresses.

## 6. Universal Design Principles for AI Coach

Synthesizing across all four cases, these design principles apply regardless of coachee type:

### 6.1 Non-Negotiable Architecture

1. **Commitment ledger with trigger conditions.** Every session must end with at least one commitment. Commitments can be active, on-hold (with named triggers), in-progress, or completed. Track across 3+ sessions.

2. **Opening grammar that adapts to session position.** Early sessions: memory demonstration + validation. Mid sessions: commitment follow-up. Late sessions: convergence synthesis. Final session: retrospective + continuation framing.

3. **Yellow lights layer.** Maintain private predictive hypotheses ("I expect X will be difficult") alongside flagged topics. Validate against future session data.

4. **Named entity tracking.** Track people, situations, and examples by name across all sessions. Use them for connection-making ("This reminds me of what happened with [NAME]...").

5. **Diagnostic data as persistent asset.** Assessment results (LSI, CB Assessment) are not one-time inputs — they are a "conversational pantry" that can be revisited when the coachee has no agenda.

### 6.2 Session Dynamics

6. **Tolerate the vacuum/bache.** When the coachee reports progress and then has "nothing else," create space rather than redirect. The deeper theme often emerges from dead spots (confirmed in 3 of 4 cases).

7. **Anticipate mid-engagement drift (sessions 3-4).** Surface issues are consumed by session 3. The AI should be prepared with: memory-based recaps, parked yellow lights, unexplored diagnostic items.

8. **Track resolution depth (DO vs. BE).** Behavioral change is not identity transformation. When the coachee reports satisfaction, check whether the root pattern has been addressed or merely accommodated.

### 6.3 Intervention Repertoire

9. **Role play for behavioral modeling.** When the coachee needs to learn a behavior they've never performed, demonstrate it using their actual context and named entities. This is a natural AI strength.

10. **Detect behavioral substitution.** When the AI offers cognitive/reflective work and the coachee returns with behavioral actions, flag the mismatch without discarding the behavioral progress.

11. **Recognize acceptance as valid resolution.** Not every gap closes through action. Conscious acceptance (changing one's relationship to the situation) is legitimate coaching outcome.

12. **Mirror cases across the arc.** When structurally similar situations recur, surface the parallel and invite the coachee to extract the meta-pattern.
