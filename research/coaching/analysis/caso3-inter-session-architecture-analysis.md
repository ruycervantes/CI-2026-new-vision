### The Opening Grammar for CASO 3

```
LSI Debrief: "Who are you? What do you see in yourself?" -> biographical context -> pre-diagnostic question -> data
CS#1:        Coachee self-directs: "I re-analyzed the report" -> self-identified pattern -> choose topic
CS#2:        Coach-led: "Here's what you committed to" -> coachee reports -> new exploration
CS#3:        Coachee-led progress report -> behavioral inventory -> then a "bache" -> late emergence of deeper theme
CS#4:        Coach-led: "Here's what was left open" -> coachee chooses -> work
CS#5:        Coachee-led: picks up the thread spontaneously -> reports actions -> work
CS#6:        Coachee-led: reports tool application results -> retrospective
```

### Key Observations

1. **Shifting ownership of the opening.** CS#1-2: the opening structure is coach-managed (coach provides the synthesis, the follow-up questions). CS#3 onward: the coachee manages the opening himself. This is a development signal -- COACHEE3 internalizes the accountability structure.

2. **CS#2 is the critical session architecturally.** COACHEE3 arrives with no pre-thought topic. Without the coach's prepared follow-up on the three commitments, the session would have no entry point. This demonstrates why commitment tracking is not optional -- it is the structural backbone when the coachee does not bring an agenda.

3. **The LSI debrief doubles as setup session.** Unlike Ruy's case (which had a separate "session 0"), CASO 3 folds context-gathering and diagnostic feedback into a single LSI session. The biographical arc (15 years, 5 role changes, the "anímicamente destrozado" moment at age 29) provides rich material that the coach can reference throughout.

4. **CS#3's "bache" is architecturally significant.** After the progress report, there was a dead spot. The deeper theme (COLEGA1) emerged only in the final minutes -- a pattern directly analogous to Ruy's Session 3 side comment. The session structure created space for this emergence precisely because the surface issues had been handled in the opening.

The escalation pattern in CASO 3 differs from Ruy's case. Ruy's commitments escalated cleanly from Action to Process to Being to Identity. COACHEE3's commitments move from Planning to Action to Interpersonal to Skill to Identity -- with more recursion. The reclamo skill is committed to in CS#4, reinforced in CS#5, and tested in CS#6. The identity-level challenge (the persistent "green" pattern) is present from the LSI debrief through CS#6 but only gets explicitly confronted in the final session.

### How the Coach Handles Non-Completion and Evasion

1. **"On hold" with trigger condition (CS#2).** When COACHEE3 put the redistribution proposal on hold, the coach did not accept passively but asked for the condition under which it would come off hold: "que tienes que ver que ocurre para sacar ese tema del 'on hold'?" This is a sophisticated form of accountability -- the commitment remains alive with a named trigger.

2. **"Ya lo intente todo" (CS#4).** COACHEE3's initial posture was learned helplessness. The coach recognized this as evasion, not truth: "quedo claro que no reclama, sino que reitera pedidos, y no reacciona ante los incumplimientos sino solo volviendo a pedir." The coach intervened with role play to demonstrate the gap between "pedir" and "reclamar" -- making the evasion concrete and visible.

3. **Green reassertion in CS#6.** The most subtle evasion: COACHEE3 settling for "tranquilidad" rather than pushing for resolution. The coach identified this as the defensive pattern re-emerging and pushed against it. This is the coach tracking the meta-pattern (green/defensive style) across the entire engagement and catching it even in its subtler forms.

### Theme Graduation

CASO 3 exhibits a clear theme graduation pattern, strikingly parallel to Ruy's case:

**Phase 1 (LSI + CS#1-2): Surface themes.**
- Workload management ("apagafuegos")
- Communication with JEFE (how to handle disagreements)
- General organization and prioritization

These are real problems but they are operational. COACHEE3 makes genuine progress on all of them.

**Phase 2 (CS#3): Transition moment.**
After reporting on surface progress, there is a "bache" -- a dead spot in the session. Then, in the final minutes, the COLEGA1 theme emerges. This is not an operational problem but an interpersonal/assertiveness challenge that directly connects to the core LSI pattern (green/defensive, avoidance, conciliation).

**Phase 3 (CS#4-6): Root cause work.**
The COLEGA1 relationship becomes the vehicle for working on the deeper pattern. The coach uses it to teach "reclamo" -- a skill that directly challenges the green/avoidance style. The arc culminates in CS#6 where the coach must confront the green pattern's reassertion even after skill transfer.

### The Late Emergence Pattern (Side Comment as Signal)

The COLEGA1 theme emerges in CS#3's final minutes -- almost as an afterthought, following a "bache." This is directly analogous to Ruy's Session 3, where the deeper theme (leadership identity) emerged from a "side comment" that Horacio recognized as "un comentario complementario... pero yo le veo una riqueza potencial interesante."

In CASO 3, the coach's recognition is equally sharp: "creo que abre una puerta grande y profunda para trabajar." The decision to advance CS#4's scheduling shows the coach recognized the urgency and importance of this emergent theme.

**Why the deep theme emerges late:**
1. Surface issues needed to be handled first (cognitive load reduction)
2. Trust had built sufficiently over 3+ sessions
3. The progress report created a sense of completion on surface issues, opening psychological space
4. The "bache" itself may have been the coachee's unconscious signal that the real issue was being avoided

### Where Practice Diverges from Theory

**1. ROLE PLAY ACTUALLY APPEARS -- and in a specific variant.**

CASO 3 is the only observed case study where role play was used. The handbook describes: (a) Direct Role Play (coachee plays self, coach plays counterpart), (b) Inverse/Houdini Role Play (coach plays coachee, coachee plays counterpart).

What Horacio used was a **"Role Play Directo, Version Modificada"** -- the coach modeled the reclamo forms directly (demonstrating what the coachee could say), rather than having the coachee practice first. This is closer to the Houdini model but adapted: the coach plays the coachee's role not to have the coachee experience the other side, but to MODEL a behavior the coachee has never attempted.

In CS#5, "micro-role plays directos" were used again -- shorter, more targeted practice rounds. This shows role play adapting to the session position: full modeling in CS#4 (first exposure), micro-practice in CS#5 (reinforcement).

**2. CS#2 has no new brecha.**

The handbook assumes each session works on a gap. CS#2 is entirely about following up on prior commitments. While this yields rich conversation (the communication strategy shift, the role acceptance), there is no new brecha. Horacio explicitly notes: "no hubo en esta sesion una brecha especifica actual nueva sobre la que hayamos trabajado." This validates that commitment follow-up sessions are legitimate coaching work even without a fresh gap.

**3. The Contrato is sometimes a carry-forward, not a negotiation.**

CS#4 and CS#5 do not negotiate what to work on -- the COLEGA1 thread carries forward as an understood contract. The formal three-objective contract (product, process, coaching) from the handbook is never explicitly used.

**4. CS#3's "bache" violates normal session flow.**

After the progress report, the session stalled. The handbook's 8-step flow assumes forward momentum (understanding leads to contract leads to resolution). Here, the momentum stopped -- and the most important theme of the engagement emerged from the dead space. The "bache" is productive precisely because it creates discomfort that forces deeper material to surface.

**5. Closing feedback only happens in CS#6.**

CS#1 has a brief emotional close. CS#4 has "agradecido y satisfecho." But structured feedback (what worked, what would change) only appears in CS#6, with specific improvement suggestions. Mid-engagement sessions close efficiently and forward-looking, not retrospectively.

## E. Praxis Principles Mapping

### The 18 Principles

#### 1. One Long Conversation
**PRESENT.** The entire engagement demonstrates this. Themes initiated in the LSI debrief (green/defensive pattern, "dilema binario") thread through all 6 sessions to the final confrontation in CS#6. The JEFE meeting commitment is tracked across CS#1, CS#2, and CS#3 before being acted on. The COLEGA1 thread spans CS#3 through CS#6. Nothing is treated as an isolated event.

Evidence: Horacio's CS#2 opening (synthesizing 3 prior commitments), CS#4 opening (carrying CS#3's emergent themes), and CS#6 closing (connecting "tranquilidad" back to the green pattern from the LSI) all demonstrate deliberate connective tissue.

#### 2. Track What's Left Out
**PRESENT.** Most clearly in the LSI debrief: "Priorizaria desarrollar la parte azul... Pero lo que el instrumento muestra como que voy por la vida solo interesado en caerles bien a los demas." COACHEE3 wants to focus on blue (constructive) development; the coach steers attention to the green (defensive) pattern that the coachee would prefer to minimize. The coach tracks what the coachee avoids.

Also present in CS#4: the coach identifies that COACHEE3's "ya lo intente todo" actually conceals that he has never truly reclaimed -- only asked repeatedly. The coach tracked what was left out of the coachee's account of his own behavior.

#### 3. Yellow Lights
**PRESENT.** Multiple examples from the coach's private notes:
- LSI debrief: "Sera fundamental ayudarlo a salir del dilema binario entre hacer lo que el otro espera o hacer lo que yo quiero sin que el otro me importe."
- CS#2: Challenge about possible manipulation ("asegurar que no se haya convertido en un manipulador rojo").
- CS#4: "Las palabras y el tono que realmente emplee para reclamar, si lo hace, mereceran nueva atencion en el futuro."
- CS#6: "La parte verde de COACHEE3 reaparecia con fuerza."

Each of these is a yellow light -- a personal reminder for future exploration that carries into subsequent sessions.

#### 4. Named Entity Tracking
**PRESENT.** Entities tracked across sessions:
- **JEFE** -- referenced in CS#1 (communication issues), CS#2 (improved 1:1 format), CS#3 (meeting scheduled for 16/7), CS#4 (reclamo target), CS#5 (escalation target)
- **COLEGA1** -- introduced CS#3, central figure CS#4-6
- **Roberto** -- introduced CS#5 as collaborator in building the action plan
- **PAIS2** -- geographic/operational context tracked from CS#4

#### 5. Everything Reveals or Conceals
**PRESENT.** CS#1: COACHEE3 says "Es como con el sicologo laboral. Me siento muy tranquilo y satisfecho, de haber abierto la mente." This comparison to a psychologist reveals the coachee's framing of coaching as emotional relief -- potentially concealing resistance to behavioral change.

CS#2: The success stories about changed communication with JEFE may reveal genuine growth but could also conceal avoidance of the harder redistribution conversation (which remains "on hold").

CS#4: "Ya lo intente todo" reveals learned helplessness while concealing that "trying everything" never included actual reclamation.

CS#6: "Tranquilidad" reveals the coachee's green comfort zone while concealing potential resignation about the COLEGA1 situation.

#### 6. Commitment as Doorway
**PRESENT.** CS#2 is the strongest example: the coachee arrived with no topic, so the coach's prepared commitment follow-up became the session's entire entry point. The three commitments from CS#1 structured the full session, yielding richer conversation than if the coachee had brought a prepared agenda.

Also present in CS#4: the COLEGA1 theme, planted as a commitment ("quedo planteado para retomarlo") in CS#3, becomes CS#4's primary material.

#### 7. Outlier vs Design
**PARTIAL.** Not as explicitly observed as in Ruy's case (where the coach asked "Was this luck or design?"). However, the coach does challenge in CS#2 whether the improved communication with JEFE might be a new form of manipulation rather than genuine growth: "asegurar que el mismo no se haya convertido en un manipulador rojo que desde el Poder o el Sabelotodo ahora convence a su jefe pero no escucha sus motivos." This serves a similar function -- probing whether the success is genuine design or a surface-level pattern shift.

#### 8. Theme Graduation
**PRESENT.** Clear graduation from surface to depth:
- CS#1-3: Workload management, apagafuegos, JEFE communication (operational)
- CS#3 (transition): Surface themes substantially resolved. Dead space. Then COLEGA1 emerges.
- CS#4-6: Assertiveness, reclamo, the green/defensive pattern (identity-level)

The surface themes did not just disappear -- they were resolved enough that psychological space opened for the deeper work.

#### 9. Side Comments as Signals
**PRESENT.** This is one of the strongest examples across all case studies. The COLEGA1 theme emerges in CS#3's final minutes, after a "bache," almost as an afterthought. The coach recognizes it immediately: "creo que abre una puerta grande y profunda para trabajar." The decision to advance CS#4's scheduling is the coach's action on this recognition.

The parallel to Ruy's case is exact: Ruy's deeper theme (leadership identity) also emerged as a side comment in Session 3. The pattern suggests that mid-engagement side comments, especially after surface progress, are reliable signals of the deeper coaching work.

This is not the "hay que" to "voy a" conversion (impersonal to personal commitment language) but it is a parallel linguistic intervention: shifting from a non-agentic verb pattern to an agentic one.

#### 17. Forced Compliance vs Flowing Integration
**PRESENT.** CS#6 is the key evidence. COACHEE3 applied reclamo with COLEGA1 and another person. With the other person it worked; with COLEGA1 it did not. ### New Praxis Principles Observed in CASO 3 (Not in Ruy's Case)

#### 19. "Bache" as Productive Emptiness
CS#3's dead spot after the progress report was not a failure of session management -- it was the structural precondition for the deeper theme's emergence. When surface issues are resolved and the coachee has "nothing left to say," the silence creates pressure that surfaces what is actually unresolved. The coach must resist the impulse to fill the space and instead wait for what emerges.

**AI Coach implication:** The AI must be able to sit with silence and non-directedness. When a coachee reports progress and then has "nothing else," the AI should create space rather than pivot to a new topic.

#### 20. Role Play as Modeling (Not Just Practice)
The handbook describes role play as practice (coachee plays self) and perspective-taking (coachee plays counterpart). CASO 3 introduces a third use: the coach plays the coachee's role to MODEL a behavior the coachee has literally never performed. COACHEE3 had never reclaimed -- he did not know what it sounded like. The coach had to show him before he could practice.

This "Modified Version" role play serves as a behavioral template transfer -- the coach demonstrates the new behavior in the coachee's context, then the coachee adapts it. This is distinct from the Houdini role play (which is about demonstrating the "best version" of the coachee) because it introduces an entirely new behavior rather than polishing an existing one.

**AI Coach implication:** The AI can demonstrate what a reclamo, a boundary statement, or a difficult conversation opening sounds like -- using the coachee's actual context and named entities. This is a powerful application of role play that an AI can deliver effectively.

#### 21. Acceptance as Resolution (Mid-Process)
CS#2: COACHEE3's acceptance that his role IS "apagafuegos" in the COVID context, combined with the 18:00 boundary, represents a form of resolution that is not about changing the situation but about changing the coachee's relationship to it. The coach validates this as legitimate progress: "transita su situacion con mas paz interior."

This is a distinct coaching outcome: the brecha closes not because the situation changed but because the coachee's stance toward it shifted. In Ruy's case, resolution came through action and role declaration. In CASO 3, one strand of resolution comes through conscious acceptance.

**AI Coach implication:** The AI must recognize acceptance as a valid resolution type, not push for action when conscious acceptance is the growth move.

#### 22. Micro-Escalation in Skill Teaching
CS#4 uses full role play to introduce the reclamo concept. CS#5 uses "micro-role plays" for refinement. This shows a deliberate pedagogical escalation: full demonstration first, then targeted practice on specific aspects. The coach adapts the teaching format to the learning stage.

**AI Coach implication:** Skill teaching should follow a similar arc -- full demonstration first, then focused micro-practice. The AI should track which skills have been introduced vs. which are being refined.

#### 23. Coach's Private Concern as Predictive Tracker
CS#4's private note ("las palabras y el tono que realmente emplee para reclamar, si lo hace, mereceran nueva atencion en el futuro") turns out to be accurate -- CS#5 confirms that the execution needed refinement, and CS#6 reveals that the skill was not yet fully integrated. The coach's private concern, recorded in notes, served as a predictive hypothesis that guided future sessions.

**AI Coach implication:** The AI should maintain a "yellow lights" layer that includes predictive hypotheses, not just flagged topics. "I expect X will be difficult for this person" should be tracked and validated against future session data.

## G. Key Observations for AI Coach Design

### 1. The "No Agenda" Session Must Be Supported

CS#2 demonstrates that a coachee may arrive with no pre-thought topic. The AI Coach must have a fallback architecture: when the coachee has nothing to bring, the commitment follow-up from the prior session becomes the session's entry point. This is not a failure mode -- it is a legitimate session type that can yield rich material. The AI must be prepared with a structured synthesis of prior commitments, ready to offer as the opening.

### 2. "Bache" Detection and Productive Silence

CS#3's dead spot preceded the engagement's most important theme emergence. An AI Coach must distinguish between: (a) a coachee who is stuck and needs prompting, (b) a coachee who has finished surface-level reporting and needs space for deeper material to surface. The temptation to fill silence with questions or suggestions may prevent the deeper theme from emerging. The AI should learn to say something like "Take your time -- is there anything else on your mind?" and wait, rather than redirecting.

### 3. Role Play for Behavioral Modeling is an AI Strength

CASO 3 shows role play used not for practice (coachee playing themselves) but for MODELING -- the coach demonstrating what a reclamo sounds like using the coachee's actual named entities and specific situation. This is something an AI can do exceptionally well: generate contextually grounded examples of assertive communication, boundary-setting, or difficult conversation openings, using the actual names and situations from the coachee's world. The "Modified Version" role play is a natural AI capability.

### 4. Pattern Reassertion Must Be Named, Not Ignored

CS#6 shows the green/defensive pattern reasserting after apparent progress. The AI Coach must track the core developmental pattern (from diagnostic data and session history) and recognize when it reappears in subtler forms. "Tranquilidad" as a form of green avoidance is not obvious -- it requires the AI to hold the meta-pattern across all sessions and compare the coachee's current behavior against it. The AI should be programmed to notice when the coachee is settling rather than striving, and to name this observation directly.

### 5. Commitment Tracking Across 3+ Sessions is Non-Negotiable

The JEFE meeting commitment was made in CS#1, remained "on hold" with a trigger condition in CS#2, and was finally activated in CS#3. The redistribution proposal was similarly tracked across multiple sessions. An AI Coach must maintain a commitment ledger that does not expire after one session -- commitments can be active, on-hold (with named trigger conditions), in-progress, or completed. The "trigger condition" pattern (from CS#2: "que tienes que ver que ocurre para sacar ese tema del 'on hold'?") is a specific mechanism the AI should replicate: when a coachee puts something on hold, ask for the condition that would take it off hold, and track that condition.
