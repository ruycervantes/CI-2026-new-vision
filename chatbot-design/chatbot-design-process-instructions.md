## Two Outputs

Every chatbot design process should produce:

**1. Project Brief**
The context document. What's the program? What's the problem or opportunity? What other tools exist? What's the gap this chatbot fills? What does success look like?

**2. Chatbot Specification**
The design document. What is this chatbot? What does it do? How does it work? What's the conversation flow? What principles guide it?

Don't draft either document until the thinking is clear.

## The Process

### Phase 1: Understand the Context

Before discussing the chatbot itself, understand what it lives within.

Ask about:
- What's the larger program or project?
- Who is it for?
- What problem are we solving or opportunity are we addressing?
- What other tools or resources already exist?
- What's the gap this chatbot would fill?
- What language or frameworks do users already know?

Capture this understandingâ€”it becomes the Project Brief.

### Phase 2: Clarify the Intent

Now focus on the chatbot itself.

Ask about:
- What type of chatbot is this? (coaching, facilitation, content generation, troubleshooting, etc.)
- What does the user arrive with? What state are they in?
- What should they leave with? What's the desired outcome?
- How prescriptive vs. emergent should it be?
- What's the chatbot's stance or orientation?

### Phase 3: Surface Design Tensions

Every chatbot has design choices with tradeoffs. Name them explicitly.

Common tensions:
- Structured flow vs. flexible conversation
- Comprehensive coverage vs. focused depth
- Expert guidance vs. facilitated discovery
- One path vs. multiple modes
- Visible framework vs. invisible scaffolding
- Specific domain guidance vs. general principles

When you identify a tension, offer options (A, B, C) and ask Ruy to choose. Don't make silent decisions.

### Phase 4: Sketch the Structure

Before drafting, propose the architecture:
- What are the modes or entry points?
- What are the phases or stages of the conversation?
- What are the key domains, categories, or dimensions?
- What principles guide the chatbot's behavior?

Ask: "Does this structure feel right?" Adjust the skeleton before fleshing it out.

### Phase 5: Draft and Iterate

Once the structure is confirmed:
- Draft the Project Brief first (context document)
- Draft the Chatbot Specification second (design document)
- Share each for feedback
- Iterate on specific concernsâ€”small adjustments, not wholesale rewrites

### Phase 6: Confirm Outputs

End with two clean documents:

1. **Project Brief** â€” capturing context, purpose, and fit
2. **Chatbot Specification** â€” capturing identity, flow, domains, and principles
