Team Effectiveness Methodology

Reference Document

Detailed frameworks, assessments, and source-by-source notes for team effectiveness methodology. Sources: Richi, Dolo, Horacio, Oseas — January 2026.

Frameworks by Source

Four consultants, four perspectives on the same methodology. Each brings unique emphasis and language.

Richi (22 Jan 2026)

Three Dimensions (I / Wi / It)

The "TO" of any gap should cover all three dimensions to create intrinsic motivation.

DimensionWhat it meansKey indicators
I (Individual)Person is inspired by the project, feels it's what they needEngagement, career fit, thriving, development, meaning in work
Wi (We)High trust, psychological safety, no undiscussablesPeople talk about everything openly, trust exists, hard conversations happen
It (Purpose)Team has its own purpose beyond company missionUnique contribution, reason to exist, team delivers business results

A team that only hits results (It) but burns people out (I) or has toxic dynamics (Wi) is not truly effective. Effectiveness means success on all three dimensions.

Three Intervention Tracks

TrackForegroundBackgroundPurpose
OffsitesPhilosophy, principles, skillsBusiness contextLearn frameworks using real business conversations
Meeting FacilitationBusinessPhilosophyCatch behaviors in flow of work, help honor what they said they wanted
Individual CoachingPersonTeam contextBuild individual skills that enable collective interdependence

Richi's language: Foreground/background flip. In offsites, philosophy is foreground. In facilitation, business is foreground and philosophy becomes the lens.

Teams as Culture Cells

  • Teams (especially leadership teams) are fundamental cells for culture
  • Culture = "how people have to think and act to belong to the group"
  • Leaders' behavior sends the strongest messages about culture
  • Don't announce rules → model behaviors first, let people ask "what's different?"

Trust-First Sequencing

Initial workshops cover:

  1. Trust-building
  2. Purpose/Why clarification
  3. Burning platform (the gap)
  4. Individual "why" connected to team "why"
  5. Operating agreements

Only AFTER trust → execution skills, accountability.

Stewardship Principle

"Yo creo que tiene que ser human led en general... necesitas el stewardship, necesitas la revisión de que eso tiene sentido."

— Dolo

Don't deploy AI blindly — maintain thoughtful human oversight. Many things CAN be automated: assessment collection, pattern analysis, AI coaching, meeting minutes, conversation prep. Human stewardship needed for: validation that outputs make sense, context interpretation, final decisions on interventions, meeting facilitation (stays human-led).

No Proprietary HPT Framework

Axialent uses public frameworks (Lencioni OR Katzenbach) for team assessment. The differentiation is:

  1. CB philosophy applied to teams
  2. Consultant judgment in combining components
  3. Facilitation skill

Teams vs. Working Groups (Katzenbach)

Before improving a team, understand: is this actually a team, or just a working group?

Working GroupTeam
Strong, clearly focused leaderShared leadership roles
Individual accountabilityIndividual AND mutual accountability
Purpose = broader organizational missionSpecific team purpose the team itself delivers
Individual work productsCollective work products
Informational meetingsProblem-solving meetings
Discusses, decides, delegatesDiscusses, decides, does real work together

"A team is a small number of people with complementary skills who are committed to a common purpose, performance goals, and approach for which they hold themselves mutually accountable."

— Katzenbach & Smith

Not every group SHOULD be a team. Sometimes a working group is the right structure. The intervention must help groups understand what level of interdependency they actually need — and build toward that, not more.

Choosing the Level of Interdependency (Sports Metaphor)

  • Swimming team (low interdependency): Individual performances, no tradeoffs, 1+1=2
  • Soccer team (high interdependency): Continuous coordination, high synergies, 1+1>2
  • Baseball team (adaptive): Plays as cohesive unit in the field, individual batters at the plate

High-performing teams operate like baseball teams — they have the flexibility to adapt and the competency to understand whether to address each task as a workgroup or a team. Some topics need full team collaboration (strategy, budgeting, people decisions). Others can be handled individually (local execution, sales). The key is making disciplined choices about when and how to work together.

Oseas (22 Jan 2026)

Commercial Framing: Team Performance, Not Coaching

"Cambia mucho la manera de poder abordar la cuestión comercial si el framing desde el principio es que tenemos una solución que se llama Team Performance."

— Oseas

Individual coaching IS the mechanism. Team Performance is the product. Same work, better positioning.

Social Context Increases Adherence

"Una de las cosas que ayudan a la adhesión del cambio de comportamiento es el entorno social."

— Oseas
  • Individual coaching alone: low adherence (like solo dieting)
  • Individual coaching in team context: higher adherence (social accountability)
  • Team dashboard showing progress: makes individual work visible

Quick Win: Team Survey + CB Assessment

Combine existing instruments:

  1. Team Survey (4-Quadrant) → WHERE team is broken
  2. CB Assessment → Individual patterns
  3. Cross-analysis → "Your team is weak on X, you're contributing via Y"

Can prototype with minimal development.

Leader-First Pathway

For dysfunctional teams:

  1. Coach the leader first
  2. Leader gains awareness + skills
  3. Leader facilitates basic team conversations
  4. Then broader team development

Theoretical Connections

Foreground/Background ↔ Activity Theory Levels

Richi's foreground/background maps to activity theory's hierarchy:

LevelWhat it isVisibility
ActivityCultural, motive-drivenUsually tacit
ActionConscious, goal-directedWhat you're paying attention to
OperationAutomatic, condition-drivenInvisible until it breaks

The hierarchy is bidirectional:

     Activity (Cultural/Tacit)
            ↑↓
     Action (Conscious/Goal)
            ↑↓
     Operation (Automatic)

Down: Learning → Practice → Automaticity. Learn a principle consciously (Action), practice until it becomes automatic (Operation), it becomes "how we do things" (Activity/Culture).

Up: What Conscious Business specifically does. Take what's tacit/automatic/cultural (invisible), bring it to consciousness (Action level). Now you can examine it, change it, choose differently.

CB's core move is bringing the tacit UP. You can't change what you can't see. The methodology makes the invisible visible.

When something is at the Operation level (automatic), you're subject to it — you can't see it. To learn/change, you must bring it to Action level (conscious) — make it object of your analysis.

This is what Richi's tracks do:

TrackWhat happensActivity theory translation
OffsitesPhilosophy is foregroundCultural level becomes conscious/actionable
Meeting FacilitationAutomatic behaviors get namedOperations become visible as actions
Individual CoachingReflect on what happenedMake automatic patterns object of analysis

"Cuando tú eres sujeto de algo, no lo ves. Para verlo tenés que moverte afuera y hacerlo objeto de tu análisis."

— Richi

This IS the activity theory move: Operation → Action, Subject → Object.

Maturity Levels by Quadrant

Each quadrant has three levels: Sub-Optimal → Standard → High Performance.

Purpose / Values / Culture

LevelCharacteristics
Sub-OptimalUnclear or disagreement about goals. No shared vision or values. No unique team purpose.
StandardClear purpose and how it supports strategy. Defined code of value-based behaviors. Agreed measures.
High PerformanceMission generates powerful shared purpose. Breakthrough objectives come from within (not externally driven). "Let's see how good we can get!"

Roles / Accountabilities

LevelCharacteristics
Sub-OptimalRoles not defined. Overlaps and gaps create disruption. Interdependencies not understood.
StandardEach role defined with right metric. Interdependencies defined. Individual contributions linked to team goals.
High Performance"My word is my bond." Mutual accountability — if one falls, all help. Wise sacrifices for team optimization.

Processes

LevelCharacteristics
Sub-OptimalInteractions unstructured and chaotic. No defined output or agenda. Communication breakdowns.
StandardEffective meetings (right topics, right people). Clear output and agenda. Appropriate communication systems.
High PerformanceFlex rules to become faster. Processes are organic, not mechanistic. "More with less" essence. After-action reviews following each project.

Relationships

LevelCharacteristics
Sub-OptimalShallow politeness covering mistrust. Silo-focused, self-promotion. Transactional exchanges only.
StandardUnderstand each other's styles. Differing viewpoints spoken and heard. Trust in competence and support.
High PerformanceNo undiscussables — everything on the table. Productive inquiry and active listening. Team members seen as friends. Attracts talent.

Accountability Framework

The Promise Framework — a framework for building accountability in the Roles quadrant.

Core Principles:

  1. Negotiated contract between two parties
  2. "My word is my bond"
  3. Countable, tangible, visible
                    Being Accountable
         +------------------+------------------+
         |  Before Promise  |  After Promise   |
         |                  |                  |
         | - Self-set goals | - Managing risk  |
         | - Anticipate risk| - Keeping score  |
         | - Make promises  | - Keeping word   |
         | - Planning       | - Stay focused   |
         | - Personal resp. | - Self-motivate  |
         +------------------+------------------+

The promise has three dimensions:

  • Objective — what will be delivered
  • Measures — how success is counted
  • Transparent — visible to all parties

Praxis Notes

Practical observations and tacit knowledge — the "how" behind the methodology.

Role Modeling Creates Moments of Truth

Source: Richi

"A través del role modeling, es como que creamos esos momentos de verdad que hacen que se ancle el aprendizaje."

— Richi

When a coach demonstrates an alternative way to say something, in the moment, it creates an "aha" that sticks. This is the tacit skill that's hardest to digitize.

Consultant Coherence

Source: Richi

"El consultor ofrece una gran coherencia interna entre lo que está diciendo y cómo se comporta."

— Richi

The coach must embody what they teach. Credibility comes from alignment between what you say and how you act. "Se comporta generando confianza, seguridad psicológica, se comporta diciendo su verdad."

Zero Attachment to Direction

Source: Richi

"Puedes hacer sugerencias, lo que no puedes es quedarte pegado a tu sugerencia."

— Richi

The coach offers perspectives tentatively. If the coachee doesn't embrace it, fine. Zero attachment to pushing in a particular direction.

Balance Investment Across Levels

Source: Horacio

If leader gets individual attention, team members should too (maybe less, but not zero). Western Union case was unusual — CEO got 6 coaching sessions, team got nothing individual. Horacio wouldn't recommend that imbalance.
